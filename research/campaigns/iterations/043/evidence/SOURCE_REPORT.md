# C043 source report: physically selected nodules and their writing

## Finding

The finite physical packet does **not supply a readable cross-function comparison**. The exact inventory joins recover one inscribed object: **HMs557 = HT Wa1018**, conventionally SI-KA (AB41–AB77). Montecchi classifies its cord arrangement as a knotted string-length, used for labeling. Neither of the two examples classified as securing two cord ends supplies a readable written element in the inspected catalogue records.

This is an object/source-control result and a failed admission gate for the proposed comparison. It does not reject or confirm an operation-code interpretation, and does not establish that SI-KA means “label.”

## Inspection and source chain

1. **Physical selection.** All four HT inventories in the author's p13 table were fixed before current exact-sign joins. The ZA object is the remaining member of the p5 cast series and remains a separately stratified comparator. `PHYSICAL_SELECTION_BEFORE_JOIN.json` preserves the initial selection and exposure. Lead froze the C043 analysis rule before this investigation joined inventories to signs.
2. **Actual physical-source pixels.** Printed pp3,5,6,13 of the complete DNB offprint were rendered and inspected. Figure3 shows HMs557's two external apertures and painted inventory; its internal knotted-length interpretation is supplied by the table, not independently demonstrated by that exterior photograph. Figures4–7 are photos of silicone casts; Figures8–11 are modern reconstructions. They are not additional ancient witnesses. Our inspection did not repeat the author's cast examination.
3. **Inventory joins.** Each selected number was searched through Arachne's public catalogue. Full exact carrier JSON was read. This distinguishes451/5 from other451 objects and546/1 from546/2. The explicit `Schriftzeichennein` fields are catalogue statements, not new autopsy observations. ZA1143 lacks that field entirely.
4. **Reading.** HMs557's explicit catalogue text ID is HTWa1018. SigLA's main HTML classifies the signs as AB41 andAB77 with `sure-reading` markup; its word view gives one SI-KA sequence. The drawing was actually viewed after compositing its transparent background on white for inspection; the original PNG is retained unchanged. The drawing includes central dotted surface/damage notation. It is a modern scholarly drawing, not an original-object photograph. The frozen corpus also has SI-KA.
5. **Primary-edition limits.** SigLA's GORILAII link points to selector68. Both the page request and a direct image request returned503, so no original GORILA target pixels were inspected. CMSII6's official digitization returned an Anubis access-denied page. No attempt was made to defeat it. Bounded search-index queries did not recover the relevant original catalogue entries. The exact catalogue negatives are therefore not newly verified against original CMS pages.

The complete physical paper is the same byte-identical source already acquired in iteration34, hash `c7bb0824b8402ebe74729cf9569a1e3022c8c7a18b1b24a7ca2a92f56744f536`. Reacquisition is maintenance. The added work is the finite inventory-to-writing join and its admission result. There is no unused-witness claim.

## Consequence for the frozen test

The readable set contains one HT object and one physical category. Thus the dependency `written mark → reconstructed local cord function` has no cross-function opportunity in this packet. Counting a one-row fit as positive support would be vacuous. Removing uncertain physical classifications or fragmentary carriers cannot create the missing readable secured-end member. Treating all catalogue negatives as unknown rather than observed uninscribed would leave the same admission failure.

A reading change on HTWa1018 alone also cannot create a comparison. The decisive missing observation is an exact readable inscription on an independently reconstructed two-cord-end securing nodule, with source and preservation controls. A new source containing such an object could justify a declared extension; adding convenient examples by sign value would require a new discovery selection rule and exposure record.
