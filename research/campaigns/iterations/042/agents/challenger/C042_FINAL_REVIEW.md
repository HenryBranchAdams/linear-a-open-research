# C042 independent final review

Reviewer: `/root/challenge`. Date: 2026-10-24. This report advises the lead; it does not amend canonical project records.

## Decision

**Accept the narrow physical constraint. Reject an inference that the packet establishes a collective consignment, an administrative action, or a participant role.** Using the inspected published capacities and preferred source numerals, KN Zb 27 and ZA Zb 3 cannot both describe the full capacity of their own single carrier in one shared volumetric unit. A version in which both jars are filled to one common fraction of capacity fails for the same reason. This conclusion does not require adopting 28.8 L or any other Linear B calibration.

The appropriate level is **reading/source control plus a conditional structural/physical constraint**. The packet does not independently establish that the numerals are volumes, that the two sites used the same unit, or that a numeral attached to a commodity sign must state a carrier's full capacity. An implicit smaller or local unit, variable partial filling, and a quantity with another scope survive. Pia de Fidio's 2024 discussion expressly gives the smaller-unit interpretation as an alternative to a collective label; it is a published, context-grounded rival.

This is a defensible improvement to this project's source control and comparative statement. The underlying size/number anomaly was already observed by Olivier in 1992. Do not report it as this phase's discovery or as previously unknown to scholarship.

## Independent calculation and its scope

Let C be carrier capacity, n the numeral and u a positive common volume unit. A full-capacity reading requires C = n·u for each carrier, hence C/n must agree. A common filling fraction f requires n·u = f·C; the same ratio condition follows, so it does not repair the incompatibility.

With preferred KN 107 and ZA 32, the nominal implied units are:

| Carrier | Published nominal capacity | Numeral used | Capacity / numeral |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| KN Zb 27 / HM 5194 | about 34 L | 107 | about 0.318 L |
| ZA Zb 3 | 556 L | 32 | about 17.375 L |

The ratio of those implied units is about 54.68. The literal ±11% tolerance published for ZA gives 494.84–617.16 L, or about 15.46–19.29 L per numeral unit in the 32 branch. If both numerals stated full capacity in one common unit, even that lower ZA capacity would require KN to hold about 1,655 L. This is a conditional calculation, not a confidence interval or a statistical rejection probability. KN's about 34 L lacks a published uncertainty interval in the inspected text; we must not manufacture one.

I independently reconstructed all nine finite numeral combinations (KN 107/117/17 × ZA 32/22/21) with exact rational arithmetic. They agree with the comparative analysis. The weakest mismatch occurs with historical KN 17 and ZA 32: the nominal implied-unit ratio is 8.6875, and KN would need about 262.88 L even at the lower published ZA tolerance. These historical branches establish sensitivity, not equal credibility for superseded readings. The reproduction is `C042_REVIEW_CALCULATIONS.py`; its output is `C042_REVIEW_CALCULATIONS.json`.

For partial filling, the requirement is only n·u ≤ C. A common unit no larger than about 0.318 L can fit both preferred readings without overflow. If KN were full, ZA's 32 units would be about 10.17 L, around 1.83% of its nominal capacity (up to about 2.06% at the lower published capacity). That would require an explanation if a full-capacity convention were independently known. Such a convention is not established by these two objects. An apparently low fill is not itself a contradiction. An intended future filling obeys the same bound; cumulative or multi-vessel quantities have a different scope.

If both vessels were full but used different units, the nominal implied-unit ratio is again about 54.68. The calculation quantifies what would be required; it does not independently disprove unit variation. The common logogram is evidence for a comparable written category, not by itself evidence for identical unit size across these contexts.

## Post hoc geometric robustness check

After the initial source and numerical review, the lead proposed an additional check because the 34 L method is unavailable. I inspected the actual HM 5194 profile in Christakis Fig. 6.2 again. Its illustrated transverse width is visibly below its height. If the published profile preserves shape, the vessel is approximately axisymmetric, and every outer horizontal diameter is no greater than the reported height of 53 cm, then its entire outer form fits inside a cylinder of diameter and height 53 cm. That cylinder holds about 116.93 L, so its internal capacity must be smaller. This is below the weakest historical requirement of about 262.88 L and far below the preferred-branch requirement of about 1,655 L.

This is a sound **conditional upper-bound calculation**, but the premises must remain attached. A single two-dimensional profile does not directly measure all horizontal diameters, and the image has no independent distortion calibration. The ordinary shape assumption is reasonable for the published jar profile, yet it is an inference from the representation. Do not recast 116.93 L as a measured capacity, a replacement for 34 L, an uncertainty bound supplied by Christakis, or independent replication. The check was proposed after seeing the mismatch and is source-led sensitivity work.

I accept its use in a technical appendix or a brief robustness paragraph with these premises. The principal result can be reported using the published approximate capacities alone. More exact geometry would not decide among a local subunit, partial filling and collective scope.

## Strongest surviving alternative and novelty challenge

The strongest grounded obstacle to a collective-label conclusion is the implicit smaller/local denomination. It has direct discussion in de Fidio 2024, and it does not require the numeral on a small vessel to count major Linear B units. The approximate 0.30 L fit to KN is suggestive of a possible scale, but without an independent unit equation or denomination marker the target cannot certify its own calibration. Variable partial filling provides a further feasible single-carrier model. These alternatives need not be historically correct to prevent the present packet from selecting collective accounting uniquely; they must be tested by evidence about units or scope.

The prefiring inscription may constrain when a mark was made. It does not prove what quantity the writer meant or establish who produced, owned, sent, received or offered the vessel or liquid. A likely imported vessel and a sanctuary find context do not bind an inscription participant. Proposed wine-offering, workshop-batch and shipment stories must remain interpretations rather than measured events.

Olivier 1992 already observed the reversed vessel-size/numeral order. Christakis 2010 already argued against a single-carrier quantity under a larger calibration. De Fidio 2024 already set out the implicit-small-unit versus collective-label choice using 107. The current contribution is a recovered reading chronology, a source-qualified reconstruction of the physical comparison, explicit premise separation, and reproducible sensitivity without importing a Linear B calibration. Do not claim a new decipherment or new-to-scholarship exclusion.

## Adjudication recommendations

1. **Accept:** with the source qualifications stated above, a common volumetric unit cannot make both preferred inscriptions equal their own carrier's full capacity; a common fill fraction has the same incompatibility.
2. **Accept with a premise label:** the enclosing-cylinder sensitivity check. Keep its geometry assumptions and post hoc status visible.
3. **Correct:** any current preferred KN numeral of 117 or inherited 17 to a scoped 107 overlay, while retaining frozen discovery bytes and historical readings.
4. **Qualify:** ZA 32 is supported by Olivier's original scholarly report but was not independently read from its definitive plate in this review. Capacity tolerance is not statistical uncertainty; KN 34 L is approximate and method-unspecified here.
5. **Reject:** “therefore a consignment,” “therefore a group of jars,” a determined wine unit, lexical meaning, an offering action or named participant role. The discriminating relationship required for those claims is missing.
6. **Correct priority:** describe the ordinal anomaly as a previously published observation newly controlled for this project. Do not count modern reanalyses as independent ancient observations or call this held-out validation.
7. **Close this bounded candidate after integration.** The remaining decisive dependency is a genuinely independent unit/scope relationship, not more arithmetic over the same two values.

## Exact next observation that could change the decision

An independently documented denomination equation or written counterpart that fixes whether KN's numeral is in a small unit, a major unit, or a group quantity would most change the interpretation. A securely linked filling mark, recorded delivery, or container/account counterpart could also establish actual rather than assumed scope. The join must identify this carrier or a documented operation, and the unit must not be inferred solely by dividing this same jar's assumed full capacity by its inscription.

A calibrated interior profile, restoration record and original capacity calculation for HM 5194 would improve the physical input and test the 34 L report. That is useful control, but by itself it cannot choose local unit against collective quantity. Better GORILA/autopsy images would improve reading control; the broad comparison already survives the finite historically reported numeral branches.
