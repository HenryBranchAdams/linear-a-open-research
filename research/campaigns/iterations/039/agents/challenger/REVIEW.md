## Consequential analytical check

The mass argument and numeral11 reading must not be collapsed. Let M=31200g denote the suggested highest unit. M/9=3466.67g: reported masses differ from this by approximately−0.48% and+1.28%. This is numerical compatibility with a selected metrological model. It does not show that the inscription contains1/9. Conversely, eleven such highest units would be343200g, around98–99 times the measured object. This motivates rejecting the literal reading that the stone's own mass equals eleven highest units under those assumptions. It does not reject numeral11, because a count of objects, account reference, or another unit can survive.

No statistical test or predictive replication was performed. These exact calculations check the argument's dependence; they do not identify a word.

## Strongest surviving alternative

Grant that the object is a genuine balance weight, possibly associated with fish. Its small inscription could still contain a phonetic designation of its owner, institution, maker or account, with AB118 part of that designation. A fish-related commodity or conventional device designation also remains possible. These explanations do not require the stone to be nonweighing and do not contradict the reported scratch rejection.

The published combination of sign shape, tablet distribution and Egyptian parallels can make a weight interpretation attractive. It does not independently bind this particular AB118 occurrence to one referent in preference to every serious local designation alternative. Morphological/semantic resemblance across cultures does not establish identical component values. Nor does an object's function alone establish that its inscription expresses the corresponding action.

The alternatives are not claimed equally probable. This review establishes an unresolved inference, not a numerical equivalence of hypotheses.
