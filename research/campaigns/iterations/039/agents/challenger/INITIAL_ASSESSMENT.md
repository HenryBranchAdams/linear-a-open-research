## Three distinct decisions

1. Does SI Zg1 have an independently supported physical weighing function?
2. If it does, which written element identifies that function, unit, commodity, user, authority, or other referent?
3. Does the object show that any Linear A construction expresses a weighing action with participants?

Success at decision 1 does not entail decision 2, and neither entails decision 3.

## Strongest live models

| Model | Object function | AB118 or associated inscription |
|---|---|---|
| W-unit | Balance weight | Weight/unit/category identifier; fish depiction may be denomination, symbol, or separate designation |
| W-commodity | Balance weight | Fish or fish-associated product designation, identifying commodity weighed rather than weight itself |
| W-authority | Balance weight | Owner, institution, maker, account or conventional phonetic designation; fish is emblem or separate mark |
| N-object | Nonweighing object or an object whose weighing use is unestablished | Fish picture and inscription identify some other purpose or referent |

These models are not all mutually exclusive. A fish-specific weight might carry both a weight sign and a fish commodity sign. The test must identify the exact target mark and compare predictions at that mark, rather than force competing whole-object paraphrases.

## What could discriminate

An exact original use context, demonstrable balance attachment/wear, an associated balance set, or a controlled series of physically calibrated masses with independent archaeological support could strengthen a weighing function without translating the inscription. Each must be checked for selection, reconstruction and postdepositional changes. A current object's known mass is an observation; its closeness to a selected unit/multiple becomes interpretation.

Even secure function leaves inscription reference open. Independent component binding could come from a controlled object series in which target-mark variation tracks known units while commodity is held fixed, or target-mark stability across independently established distinct commodities and calibrated roles. Independently established same referent in another medium might constrain a category. A bilingual or explicit ancient equivalence could be decisive. No such evidence is assumed available.

A specialist's typological classification can be valuable evidence if its diagnostic basis is supplied and independent of the inscription. It cannot be treated as a directly witnessed ancient weighing event.

## What does not discriminate by itself

- The catalogue or 3D page calls it a weight.
- Its mass fits some convenient integer multiple or fraction after selecting a standard.
- It resembles Egyptian objects called fish weights while the ancient Egyptian texts establish use only for those Egyptian objects.
- The sign looks pictorially like a balance, fish, vessel or unit.
- A newer 3D model, clean drawing or enhanced view confirms an inscription but not its referent.
- AB118 is associated with numbers on tablets, without a direction or object-function binding.
- Identifying one neighboring mark as AB01 rather than numeral 11, unless an explicit rival prediction turns on that difference.
- Multiple modern discussions repeat the same edition or the same mass measurement.

## Source-review requirements and stop

Review original publication's full argument at the exact target pages/figures; distinguish source wording from our inference; verify exact museum inventory and inscription face; retain alternate readings and figure limitations. Compare Egyptian analogies for morphology, mass, marks and actual inscription content, without transferring meaning across cultures automatically.

If the finite packet improves reading/physical control but lacks a component-bound discriminator, verdict should be “needs narrowing; semantic assignment unresolved.” If it establishes weighing typology independently, report that narrow support without upgrading it to an action-and-participant construction. Stop when obtainable decisive dependencies are exhausted. No arbitrary witness minimum is imposed.
