# C044 — bounded ceramic-rival check

Author: closure_sources agent. Handoff to lead and independent challenger, 25 October 2026 (phase date, America/Chicago). This is supporting source work, not a separate campaign or a new semantic claim.

## Result

**No inspected ceramic object in the finite packet supplies the combination of rounded shallow basin, upright loop handles and long straight supports depicted by HT31.1.** The exact kiln waster is meaningful evidence for ceramic tripod production, but its preserved features do not match the whole written form. A second, same-site tripod lead is a perforated tray/support with a different body and feet. The direct ceramic comparison cited by Duhoux remains inaccessible at full-text level. This result does not establish that no ceramic counterpart exists, or that the written category necessarily excludes ceramic versions.

The positive bronze comparison therefore survives this check. Its strength is resemblance to an actual archaeological object, not merely the three-legged outline. The additional inference from a recognizable bronze prototype to an exclusively bronze denotation still needs its own premise; the present evidence neither proves nor refutes that premise.

## Three rival packets and stops

### 1. Puglisi2011: c.29a1

I independently read the primary catalogue text supplied through web extraction of the exact author-posted article URL. The inspected campaign extraction is recorded by the lead as `turn212view0` / `turn213view0`. Context5 consists of material from around the first pillar (c.29a) and cleaning of the first eastern channel (c.30c), assigned securely by Puglisi to the firing-chamber area. The catalogue calls c.29a1 a waster and describes blackened cooking fabric. This is reported archaeological production evidence; I performed no material examination. Body and handle morphology are sufficient to reject its use as an already established exact A410 counterpart. The omitted legs are **unpreserved**, not evidence that the original vessel lacked them. Stop: figures remain unavailable; the accessible catalogue does not supply the missing diagnostic structures. A complete specimen or image showing those structures would be a new packet.

### 2. Duhoux2003: direct ceramic cross-reference

Actual pixels of Duhoux2000–2001 pp39–40 confirm that his argument acknowledges terracotta tripods and specifically relies on the drawn legs' length and thinness. The reference “DUHOUX2000 §4.1.2” resolves through the author's bibliography to **Yves Duhoux2003, “Un nom du ‘bronze’ en linéaire A ?”, Briciaka / Cretan Studies9,17–26**. This is a bibliographic resolution, not article inspection.

The author-posted ResearchGate record advertises a public full text uploaded 20 September 2018. The tool's advertised PDF link resolves through a malformed relative path (404); the corrected public link returned DisabledError. A direct request for the article page returned403. A bounded exact-title search located bibliographies and the already acquired Minos paper, not another full-text route. Stop: **§4.1.2 remains uninspected**. It is the highest-value obtainable source request from this check, because it may identify exact ceramic comparanda that Duhoux himself considered.

### 3. Puglisi2010: tripod tray

The author-posted indexed text supplies enough morphology to close this candidate as a close A410 match: a flat perforated platform and flattened triangular supports differ from the target's basin and long line-like feet. The main discussion identifies HTR1036, whereas the extracted Fig8 caption and English summary give HTR1060; the latter also labels a separate ceramic disc. No silent equivalence is made. Original figure pixels were not obtained, so the discrepancy is recorded as a **source text-layer inconsistency**, not definitively diagnosed as a printed error. Stop: explicit body morphology fails the intended comparison; no wider tray survey is warranted.

## Adjudication recommendation

Retain a **positive, local iconographic bronze-class comparison**. Do not claim that the generic existence of ceramic tripods defeats Duhoux: he already knew that and made a narrower proportional argument. Do not promote c.29a1 to an exact matching ceramic specimen. Equally, a finite unsuccessful counterpart search is not an archaeological absence result. A conclusion that HT31 cannot concern the kiln's ceramic output remains conditional on the sign's category being restricted to bronze and on the full list sharing that production scope. No action, direction, owner, maker or recipient is identified by this check.

The most informative next observation is Duhoux2003 §4.1.2 with its cited ceramic examples and figures, followed only if warranted by one exact specimen preserving basin, handles and supports. Puglisi2011 Fig34 alone may improve the waster's body/handle control, but cannot be assumed to restore structures absent from the fragment.
