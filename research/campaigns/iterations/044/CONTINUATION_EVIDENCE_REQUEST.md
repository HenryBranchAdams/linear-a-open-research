## Request A — Duhoux's exact ceramic comparanda

**Subject:** Source request: Duhoux2003, “Un nom du ‘bronze’ en linéaire A ?”, pp17–26

Dear colleague,

We are checking the archaeological comparison underlying the first vessel sign on Linear A tablet HT31. Would you be able to provide a readable copy, or an authorized access route, for:

Yves Duhoux, **“Un nom du ‘bronze’ en linéaire A ?”**, in Yves Duhoux (ed.), *Briciaka. A Tribute to W.C.Brice*, **Cretan Studies9** (Amsterdam: Adolph M.Hakkert, **2003**), **pp17–26**?

The specific dependency is the discussion of ceramic tripod vessels cited as **“DUHOUX2000, §4.1.2”** in Duhoux, *“Un inventaire linéaire A de vases: la tablette HT31”*, **Minos35–36 (2000–2001), p40**. The earlier citation uses the article's in-press year; the author's bibliography records its publication in2003. We have not inspected the published article and therefore cannot confirm whether its section numbering remained unchanged.

We need the relevant section **together with its notes, references and any figures**, preferably within the full ten-page article so that the comparison's scope and qualifications remain visible. If the discussion identifies ceramic specimens, the most useful details are their exact inventory or catalogue identifiers, archaeological context/date, and the cited publication pages or plates. Our question concerns the combination of body shape, handle orientation and support proportions, so an isolated statement that ceramic tripods existed would not resolve the source dependency.

The public author-posted ResearchGate record is identifiable as publication **327727807**, but its advertised full-text route was not accessible in our research environment. We would welcome a lawful copy or another authorized route; no special interpretation or translation is requested.

Thank you for any assistance with the source.

## Request B — exact kiln fragment, original figures and a documented complete profile

**Subject:** Source/object request: Puglisi2011, kiln catalogue5.3 / excavation c.29a1

Dear colleague,

We are checking whether a particular ceramic vessel from the Haghia Triada kiln can serve as a morphological comparator for the first vessel sign on tablet HT31. We would be grateful for readable reproductions, or an authorized access route, for the following parts of:

Dario Puglisi, **“La fornace da vasaio TM IB di Haghia Triada. Le ceramiche e il sistema di produzione, distribuzione e consumo”**, **Creta Antica12 (2011)**:

- **p215, Fig8c**, including its full caption and scale information;
- **p255, catalogue5.3**, including the object description and any associated notes;
- **p256, Fig34**, with a readable key, caption and scale, and a clear indication of the element corresponding to catalogue5.3;
- the **context5 firing-chamber description on p253**, if a complete article copy is unavailable.

Our target is specifically **catalogue5.3 = excavation code c.29a1**, described as a tripod cooking-pot waster. The accessible catalogue text reports a body fragment with half a horizontal cord handle and a cylindrical body. We do not treat the excavation code as a museum inventory number, and we do not infer unpreserved feet from the fragment itself.

If held and readily identifiable, a photograph or drawing of the **exact fragment** would help us check its surviving profile and handle attachment. Please distinguish preserved contours from restored outlines. Any current collection/inventory identifier should be accompanied by the documented cross-reference to **c.29a1**, rather than inferred from a nearby entry or similar vessel.

If the classification as a tripod cooking pot rests on a documented join, reconstruction or a specific sufficiently complete comparator, we would also welcome the **single most diagnostic record**: its specimen identifier, source page/plate, scale/profile, and archaeological context/date. The needed structures are the body, handle orientation, and the length, thickness and attachment of the supports. A complete vessel of broadly similar function is useful only if the grounds for assigning it to the same morphological class are documented. If no such record or complete counterpart is known, that limited answer would also help us preserve the scope of the evidence accurately.

We are requesting records and images, not a proposed reading of HT31 or an assessment of what its administrator intended.

Thank you for any assistance with the evidence.

## Why these observations matter

This explanatory note accompanies the research record; it need not be included in either outgoing request.

The currently inspected sign has a rounded shallow body, upright loop-like handles and three long straight supports. Malia bronze vessel inv3068 supplies a close archaeological analogue. The surviving c.29a1 fragment establishes local ceramic tripod production at the level reported in the catalogue, but does not currently supply the target's whole form. These are separate propositions.

| New observation | Effect on the comparison |
|---|---|
| A documented ceramic specimen of relevant date preserving the rounded basin, upright handles and comparably long slender supports | Would provide a serious material rival and weaken the inference that the sign's depicted form requires bronze. The positive bronze resemblance would remain, but would cease to distinguish the two materials by itself. |
| Duhoux's cited ceramic examples are materially different in those precise features, and his argument records the differences explicitly | Would strengthen the source-based rationale for the bronze comparison within the examples actually considered. It would not establish the absence of every ceramic counterpart. |
| Original c.29a1 figures confirm only cylindrical wall and horizontal handle, with no documented join or complete profile | Would strengthen the conclusion that this exact waster is insufficient as a close A410 counterpart. It would not show that ceramic tripod vessels as a class were incompatible. |
| A documented join or same-class complete profile establishes that the local ceramic form had the target's body, handles and supports | Would make the local production rival substantially stronger than the surviving fragment alone permits. The join/classification and material would need independent documentation. |

None of these observations would, by itself, identify HT31 as an order, receipt, kiln batch or mixed inventory, or assign a maker, supplier or recipient to its written header. That additional administrative relationship requires separate evidence connecting the record to an event or scope.
