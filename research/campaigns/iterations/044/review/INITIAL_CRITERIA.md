# C044 initial challenge criteria

## Exposure at freeze

I have read C044 BRIEF and the lead's message identifying a reported metal-vessel argument in Militello2011 and a proposed ceramic tripod waster in Puglisi2011 (catalogue5.3/c.29a1/context5). I have not yet inspected the two sources, target figures or the lead's preferred synthesis. The potential rival is already exposed, so this is a prospective review plan within a retrospective candidate investigation, not a blind ancient-data test.

## Exact argument and identity

1. Read the original author's claim in its complete sentence and nearby qualifications. Distinguish a categorical exclusion from a plausibility preference, and a legs-only argument from a diagnosis based on body, rim, handles or typological lineage.
2. Verify which HT31 sign is being interpreted, its reading uncertainty and actual morphology. A410VAS is an editorial label, not an ancient material word. Inspect original/edition photograph and drawing separately where accessible.
3. Verify Puglisi5.3/c.29a1/context5 through the catalogue, caption, figure and context description. Distinguish recovered ceramic sherd/complete restored vessel, waster designation, firing-chamber find, reconstruction and known production stage. A nearby tripod or an illustration with the wrong inventory cannot be substituted.

## Strong comparison

4. Compare vessel features individually: bowl/body openness, rim form, handle placement/orientation/type and supports. Three legs alone do not settle material, but neither does the existence of any ceramic tripod refute a more specific metallic cauldron diagnosis.
5. Steelman the metal interpretation: a particular combination of vertical ring handles, broad bowl and thin high legs could point strongly to a metal type even if ceramics sometimes have three feet. Assess the published type and its closest real ceramic rival.
6. Allow schematic compression. Neither exact scale/proportions nor every technical feature must be pictured. Explain which visible traits are discriminating at the image's resolution; do not add invisible metal shine, rivets, wall thickness or ceramic fabric.
7. If material is not uniquely identifiable, report the resulting comparative support and uncertainty. A ceramic rival can weaken a categorical exclusion without proving a ceramic denotation or identifying the kiln as the list's source.

## Event and scope

8. Separate repertoire compatibility from an exact production/order/receipt event. Verify where HT31 was found and its relationship to the kiln. Same site and broad period do not establish same batch; a waster proves a manufacturing failure only at its identified context and stage.
9. A mixed-material inventory survives a ceramic option. A pottery-production account survives a nonexclusive metal iconography only as a hypothesis. Neither the header nor actor/theme roles acquire meaning from the sign's shape alone.
10. Do not convert the quantitative resemblance of a kiln assemblage and list into a join unless counts, preservation and accounting scope are independently constrained. No unnecessary fitted model or significance calculation.

## Review outcome

Return a source-specific feature matrix and an explicit adjudication of the actual published argument. If the diagnostic features retain a strong metal preference, state it; if only the categorical exclusion fails, narrow the result accordingly. No new translation or mission completion without a separate independent operational/participant link. Recommend further inspection only where an attainable image/source can change that comparison.
