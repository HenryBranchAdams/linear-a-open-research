# C044 independent review and adjudication

## Verdict and level reached

**Accept a graded local preference for A410 as a bronze-cauldron vessel class.** The independent archaeological comparison supports the combination of a rounded basin, upright loop handles and long narrow supports. The actual local ceramic waster proposed as a rival does not reproduce that combination. The result has therefore advanced beyond source/object control to a **conditional local category/material discrimination**.

Of the lead's three proposed levels, this is level **(c), graded local denotational preference**, if the claim explicitly retains the premise that the sign's diagnostic pictorial features constrain its referent. The evidence establishes the pictured type more securely than the material extension of the counted category. It does not establish a context-free reading “bronze,” a rule that A410 can count only bronze objects, or a linguistic action-and-participant construction. The administrative consequence is level (b): a conditional constraint on an exclusively ceramic kiln consignment.

## Decisive reconstruction

The inspection chain is preserved in C044_RECONSTRUCTION.md, with a feature matrix and exact source scopes. The decisive original pages are Vandenabeele1974 pp8–10, p18 fig2 and p21 fig21; Duhoux2000–2001 pp38–40; Militello2011 pp274–285; and Puglisi2011 context5/catalogue5.3 in web-extracted author article text. The local Puglisi2011_Academia.html is the author landing page, not a downloaded full publication PDF or a page-image edition.

The actual HT31 photograph and drawing show a basin on three long straight supports with two upright loops. The published Malia Quartier Mu inventory3068 photograph supplies a close independently recovered bronze counterpart. It has three handles, whereas the sign has two; Vandenabeele explicitly records that difference. This is a typological relationship, not identity with the vessels counted on HT31.

Duhoux does not reason merely that all tripods are bronze. He acknowledges ceramic tripods and points to the length and thinness of the sign's supports. Puglisi5.3 = c.29a1 is a reported ceramic firing waster from context5. Its preserved part is a wall fragment with half of a horizontal cord handle, and its body is cylindrical. The described fragment has no preserved legs. It is secure evidence for a ceramic tripod-pot production class at the site, but it cannot test the support diagnosis and differs in the other surviving features. Its actual figure remains uninspected.

The finite follow-up to this comparison recovered no closer ceramic counterpart. I read its final report and access ledger after the pre-follow-up reconstruction. The source investigator's Puglisi2010 tripod-tray candidate has a substantially different disc-and-feet arrangement and a text-layer identity inconsistency (body HTR1036; caption/summary HTR1060); it is not admitted as a target match. I have not independently inspected that article's figures. The chapter cited as DUHOUX2000 resolves to the author's 2003 *Un nom du “bronze” en linéaire A ?*, Briciaka/Cretan Studies9,17–26; §4.1.2 remains uninspected after the recorded public access routes. Citation year and publication year are preserved separately. These are bounded packet outcomes, not evidence that no matching ceramic form existed.

## Strongest surviving alternative

A material-neutral conventional vessel category remains the strongest rival to **exclusive bronze denotation**. Militello2011 p278 explicitly entertains inherited or standardized graphic forms. This provides a reason to separate a metal-derived pictorial prototype from the material of every counted referent.

Its support is limited: the inspected material does not demonstrate this exact A410 sign used for independently identified ceramic objects. Nor does the handle-number discrepancy demonstrate cross-material use. The rival therefore deserves a qualification, not equal evidential weight. The bronze-cauldron preference remains stronger within the bounded comparison.

There is also a technical limit on Duhoux's exclusion argument. A single incised line is not a physical measurement of a leg's thickness. The combination of proportions, body and handles is stronger evidence than line width treated as metallurgical necessity. No physical impossibility test for ceramic supports was performed, and no such universal claim is admitted.

## Administrative consequence

The result constrains this proposition: **the complete HT31 list records only ceramic vessels produced in the specified kiln consignment**. It is disfavored if A410 retains a bronze-restricted referent. The inference is conditional because an exclusive material rule and the tablet-to-consignment relationship have not been independently established.

Militello's concluding objection concerns a kiln order or receipt. His abstract p285 still considers broader acquisition or distribution. A bronze item can occur in a mixed-material inventory, acquisition, allocation or issue. The evidence does not select among those actions or identify who ordered, produced, received, issued or owned the vessels.

The kiln fragment's exact context cannot be transferred to HT31. Militello's Room59 attribution for the tablet is qualified, and the inspected packet provides no exact tablet-to-batch join. Repertoire and firing-capacity compatibility therefore do not identify a transaction.

## Review independence and disposition

The general and C044-specific criteria were preserved before the relevant outcome inspection; their original files remain unchanged. I knew that the ceramic waster was the proposed rival before reading the original sources. I had not read a preferred lead synthesis when constructing this review. Vandenabeele → Duhoux → Militello is a source-dependence chain, while Malia3068, HT31 and c.29a1 are different ancient objects. Separate reviewers do not multiply those objects or create unused validation.

The new result for this investigation is that the apparently damaging local ceramic rival fails the exact feature comparison and the bronze-class preference has an independently inspectable archaeological basis. This recovers and tests an existing scholarly argument; it is not a newly discovered ancient counterpart or a new translation. Earlier “Militello2011 unread” register entries concern a different Pasiphae article and remain unaffected.

**Candidate stop:** the declared comparison and finite follow-up are complete. Further generic searches for ceramic tripods have low decision value. Reopen for a securely identified ceramic object matching the combined features, a target-specific cross-material use of A410, or an exact tablet-to-vessel/event relationship. The first could alter material preference; the last is required to move toward an action and participant construction.
