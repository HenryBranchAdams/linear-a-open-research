# C044 independent reconstruction

Reviewer: independent_challenge. Status: original packet assessed; the separate, finite follow-up to Duhoux2000's ceramic-tripod discussion is pending. This file records the assessment before that follow-up and before any preferred lead synthesis.

## Outcome from the inspected packet

The bronze interpretation survives the proposed ceramic-waster challenge. A410 on HT31 has a rounded basin, upright loop handles and three long narrow supports; these features jointly resemble the published bronze cauldron Malia, Quartier Mu, inventory3068. The local ceramic waster Puglisi5.3 has a cylindrical body and a horizontal cord handle. Its surviving wall-and-handle fragment does not preserve the supports that would test Duhoux's specific diagnosis. The existence of this ceramic tripod therefore does not refute the bronze-cauldron identification.

The strongest result is a **graded local preference for a bronze-cauldron vessel class**, under the explicit premise that these pictorial features retain their referential distinction on HT31. This is stronger than access or object control alone. It is weaker than an established rule that every vessel counted under A410 was made of bronze. The implication for the full tablet is consequently conditional: if A410 has a bronze-restricted referent and the proposed consignment contains only this kiln's ceramic output, that consignment interpretation fails. Neither condition identifies an administrative action or participant.

## What is and is not discriminated

### 1. The sign's pictorial prototype

The Malia cauldron explains the combined visible features better than the particular ceramic-pot class represented by Puglisi5.3. This is a positive comparison, not an argument from absence of all ceramic tripods. It would be a mistake to reject the primary bronze interpretation on the sole ground that ceramic tripods existed: Duhoux explicitly knew this, and the original comparison includes more than three legs.

### 2. Material of the counted referents

Depicting a recognizably metal-derived type and restricting the counted category to metal are related propositions, but they are not identical. A conventional vessel sign could retain an older shape or name a broader class. Militello's p278 warning about graphic persistence grounds this as a serious mechanism. The inspected packet, however, contains no target-specific case in which this exact glyph demonstrably counts ceramic vessels. That rival warrants a qualification; it does not warrant treating the bronze and ceramic interpretations as equally supported.

The long-support argument also relies on pictorial fidelity. Single-line engraving is not a direct measurement of ancient leg thickness. Relative support length and the combined shape are more secure observations than inferred metallurgical necessity from line width alone. No experimental structural limit for ceramic legs has been demonstrated here, and none is needed merely to prefer the closer archaeological class. It would be needed for a universal physical-impossibility claim.

### 3. Administrative scope and direction

Militello's categorical last objection concerns a kiln order or receipt. His English abstract p285 still permits a broader order for acquisition or distribution. It would misrepresent him to report that a bronze vessel excludes all orders or receipts.

The bronze interpretation is compatible with a mixed-material inventory, acquisition, allocation or issue. These can contain ceramic vessels produced at this kiln while also containing metal vessels from another source. Conversely, removing the bronze restriction would merely remove one objection to a kiln interpretation; it would not supply the missing tablet-to-batch or action-to-participant join.

HT31's attribution to the Room59 group is qualified by Militello as approximate. Puglisi's c.29a1 context is more specific, but it belongs to the ceramic fragment. Transferring that specificity to the tablet would manufacture a join. Similar site, period, repertoire and feasible firing capacity do not establish an order, delivery, receipt or exact batch.

## Decision and next observation

Recommend reporting a **graded local denotational/category preference**, with an explicit boundary: A410 is better supported as a bronze-cauldron type than as the tested ceramic-pot class; an exclusive bronze-material extension is not independently confirmed. If the project taxonomy reserves semantic discrimination for confirmed material restriction, classify the achieved result as a conditional material/scope constraint and retain the preference in the claim wording. Access-only understates the positive morphology; a categorical semantic reading overstates it.

The next source that could most change the material decision is a securely identified ceramic comparator matching the rounded basin, upright loops and long narrow supports, or a documented case showing this sign's referential range independent of its image. Better imagery of c.29a1 can verify its preserved body/handle, but cannot reveal legs that are absent. An action-and-participant result additionally requires a tablet-to-container, consignment, storage or production event link with independent direction or role information.

A finite follow-up to the exact Duhoux2000 ceramic-tripod discussion is being pursued by the source team. Append its result before final adjudication. No general search for any three-legged ceramic vessel is needed.
