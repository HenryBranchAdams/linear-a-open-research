# C044 decisions and exposure

## Admission and initial question

C043's physical packet failed its readable-comparison gate. The independently obtained HT31/kiln articles supplied a different accessible question: whether a real ceramic tripod waster undermines the bronze identification used to exclude the full vessel list from one ceramic kiln's output, order or receipt. The brief was saved after this initial discovery and before detailed original morphological comparison. It is retrospective investigation, not unused validation.

## Source-led refinement

The original chain changes the comparison. Duhoux 2000–2001, p. 40, explicitly acknowledges ceramic tripods. His material argument concerns the legs' length and thinness. Vandenabeele 1974, pp. 8–10 and figs. 2 and 21, adds the curved basin and upright handles, comparing HT31.1 with the excavated bronze Malia, Quartier Mu, inventory 3068. A generic three-legged ceramic pot therefore does not refute the strongest published argument.

The ceramic waster is nevertheless useful independent production evidence: Puglisi 2011 catalogue 5.3, excavation c.29a1, context 5. Its description preserves a cylindrical wall and horizontal cord handle, without legs. Its figure pixels remain unacquired. The lead and reviewer read the actual web-extracted author text; the separately downloaded HTML does not contain that extracted text and is not a local full-text preservation claim.

## Distinctions fixed before ceramic follow-up

1. What the glyph depicts: a vessel with a particular combination of features.
2. Which material the depicted class favors: actual bronze morphology versus particular ceramic comparators.
3. Whether the sign's local extension was restricted to metal referents: an additional inferential bridge, not direct material observation.
4. Whether the whole list is the exclusive output of one ceramic kiln: a further scope premise.
5. Whether the record is an order, receipt, inventory or issue, and who participated: no independently joined event yet.

Militello 2011, p. 278, explicitly discusses persistent graphic conventions. This makes an inherited type sign a grounded caution, but does not itself demonstrate a ceramic referent of A410. The two drawn handles versus three on the bronze are preserved. The later authors depend on Vandenabeele; three publications do not equal three independent ancient witnesses.

## Follow-up boundary

The source investigator tests at most three concrete ceramic-source packets from the cited chain and exact same-site catalogues. Generic tripod existence, ambiguous inventories and another inaccessible mirror do not justify an unbounded search. The reviewer reconstructs the direct comparisons before the lead's preferred synthesis. Any resulting preference will be graded and local; no PU-KO translation or administrative direction is authorized by morphology alone.
