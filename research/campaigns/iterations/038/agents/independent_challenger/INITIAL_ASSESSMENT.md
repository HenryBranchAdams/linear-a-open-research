## Neutral criterion

The question is whether an exactly identified written component can be connected to an independently established operation or relationship strongly enough that two serious explanations predict different observations. Evidence that an object was sealed, attached, fired, moved, opened, stored, or deposited does not by itself identify what its writing denotes. A source can establish a physical participant without establishing that participant as the inscription's referent.

I will use the following chain as a check, not as assumed facts:

1. Exact object, image, surface, and written component.
2. Reading and damage alternatives sufficient for the proposed comparison.
3. Independently supported physical operation or relationship.
4. An independent binding between that relationship and the written component.
5. A prediction that changes under the strongest serious rival.
6. A justified local result, followed separately by any generalization or linguistic claim.

If changing responsible party to recipient, individual to office, or transaction direction preserves all observations under equally plausible contextual assumptions, the role remains unidentified. Broad roles may overlap; the actual comparison must be narrowed before claiming exclusion. A merely possible story is insufficient as a rival, but absence of a discriminating observation cannot be repaired by preferring one fluent paraphrase.
