## What the comparison now tests

The initial attraction was straightforward: if two similar containers carry different marks, perhaps the marks distinguish their contents. That hypothesis becomes useful only if differences in manufacture, intended use and capacity have been adequately controlled. Here the newly acquired physical evidence reveals that manufacture varies alongside the marks.

| Interpretation | Differentiating prediction | Current evidence and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One fixed prominent mark determined solely by coarse vessel type | All G10 examples in the proposed local code have the same mark | The pair has different preserved codes within the author's G10 class. This restricted deterministic mapping fails if those classifications are accepted. |
| Contents or intended function | Within independently grouped production classes, marks follow independently established content/use | Content/use is unknown for both; manufacture is not held constant. Not selected. |
| Production or workshop group | Marks follow independently established production groups when content/use is controlled | Technical differences give this rival a positive basis. Two objects, no independently established workshop groups and unknown contents do not identify it. |
| Capacity-related convention | Marks correlate with independently measured capacities or standards | One measured volume and an inferred second volume cannot test the pair's equality. Broad capacity interpretations remain open. |
| Planned recipient/allocation | A production-stage designation tracks an independently linked recipient or order | Prefiring marking permits this mechanism; no exact recipient/order link was recovered. |

This is not a claim of equal probabilities. Production-related labeling is now a physically motivated rival to the contents preference. The evidence does not decide between them, because technical group, content and intended use have not been crossed independently.

The fixed-type-code result is structural. It does not refute every vessel-name interpretation: subtypes, different names or acrophonic conventions and an incomplete designation can survive. Likewise, matching rims do not prove matching whole-body volumes. No numerical significance test or invented probability score is appropriate for this selected two-vessel packet.

### The consequential operator correction

The 2021 argument treated tablet-like writing as establishing a distinction between potter and inscriber. The later paper explicitly says that separation is no longer necessary and offers either a community/workshop scribe or a potter who wrote. [S1p286; S2p13]

We accept the correction at that scope. It removes a claimed necessity, without proving either that the potter wrote or that a separate scribe did. A physical production stage does not identify the historical operator, and an operator does not automatically become the inscription's referent.
