## Admission finding

This is potentially different evidence from a sign merely placed on a pot: an internally modified vessel ideogram might denote a fractional standard. But the needed independently measurable relation is not recovered. The carrier is described as fragmentary; no exact measured capacity, independently fixed base-unit vessel or object-specific calibration accompanies the inspected passages. Choosing a unit to fit K would fit the hypothesis rather than independently test it. K's numerical value also must not be imported from an older paper as settled without the project's retained branches.

The relevant rivals remain a fraction of a standard measure, a quantified commodity/account designation, a vessel classification or another inventory convention. Difference between the carrier's shape and the depicted vessel weakens a literal drawing-of-this-pot account; it does not uniquely establish capacity or the inscription's referent. No participant role is independently bound.

**Verdict:** stop at screening, as directed by the lead. Recovering the original Perna/Pelon argument, exact inventory, complete carrier reconstruction and a genuinely independent calibration would justify fresh intake. Another citation repeating that the mark denotes capacity does not. No dormant A588 claim is reopened, no semantic experiment admitted, and no new scientific result proposed. This is a finite access/feasibility boundary, not refutation of the capacity hypothesis.
