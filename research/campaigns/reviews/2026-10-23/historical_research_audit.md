# Historical research review for the consolidation through iteration 41

**Review date:** 23 October 2026. **Scope:** cumulative research through investigation 37, with a check that the controlling historical documents remain preserved in the iteration 41 package. This is a documentation review, not a new research iteration or an independently executed decipherment experiment.

## Decision

The 18 October baseline is a sound historical control layer. Its central conclusion remains appropriate: no project result has established a Linear A construction whose linguistic action and relevant participant roles are independently supported. The record does contain useful reading corrections, accounting structures, narrowly specified model exclusions, and physical production constraints. Those results should remain visible without being promoted to meanings or roles. [B1; B2]

The baseline's claim dispositions, history crosswalk, review coverage, exposure ledger, and baseline report are byte-identical in the original baseline package and the iteration 41 package. Their preservation therefore appears deliberate. This check establishes continuity of those five files, not the correctness of every historical observation. [Local comparison performed in this review]

The most important documentation risk is **unqualified reuse of original reports whose conclusions or proposed next steps were later narrowed**. For example, iteration 15 calls a secure HT100 numeral semantically decisive; the controlling review says it distinguishes aggregate inclusion from exclusion, which still does not select a lexical meaning. Iteration 16 calls HT34 and HT118 semantic breakthroughs; later review, iteration 17, and iteration 21 explicitly narrow those claims. Originals should stay intact, but the current reading order must place their controlling corrections beside them. [H15; H16; H17; H21; B2, B-D05/B-D06/B-D09]

## 3. Critical claim chains to keep immediately accessible

### TA-NA forms: source control without a shared-referent bridge

The inspected round-9 report keeps complete TA-NA-TE and A-TA-NA-TE on ZA10a separate from complete TA-NA-TI on HT7a, HT10b, and HT98a. Its strict search for complete independent target-face associates found no bridge. Whole-tablet aggregation produces SA-MA only on the opposite faces. The result is a bounded failure to find the bridge, not a finding that the forms definitely identify different entities. TE/TI remains site-confounded in this selected complete set. [H9 §§1–2; B2 B-D17]

ZA9's final sign remains unresolved, with possible TE in the apparatus. A future secure TI could weaken the site confound without independently establishing shared identity or case meaning. Linear B KN Uf311 is left-broken and the object missing; it is not a complete ta-na-ti name paired with ta-na-to. The ordinary Greek o-stem model predicts the same -to spelling for the proposed nominative/dative comparison. External type support does not determine Linear A participant roles. [H9 §§2–3]

**Reopen for:** better diagnostic surface evidence or complete independently linked same-designation contrasts, followed by an independently discriminating context. Do not reopen merely to rematch the same spellings. [B2 B-D17]

### HT100: numerator reading and meaning are different questions

The two modeled branches are exact: a restored 4 includes KI 2 in 97, while a restored 6 leaves the other entries already totaling 97. The source review did not select either reading. A new numeral could resolve that aggregate branch; it would not on its own translate KI or establish debt polarity. The original 14–15 future-work rhetoric is too strong unless read with the later limitation. [H14 Lane C; H15; B2 B-D09]

**Reopen for:** relevant new reading evidence and an independent operation/role context. If a new reading is the only gain, report a reading or scope result. [B2 B-D09]

### HT94b and the KI-RO lists

Iteration 13 calls the list a personnel construction and prints complete KU-RO 5. Iteration 14 narrows entry ontology to labels. Iteration 21 still prints the complete 5 in its comparison table; iteration 22 and the baseline control that table with the source-broken **5[**. HT88's six entries and HT117a's ten remain the complete list-closure examples. Neither the unit figures nor personnel signs elsewhere on an object independently type every label as a person. [H13; H14 Lanes A–B; H21 §3; H22 cycle 22; B2 B-D04]

### HT13 and the circular-order result

HT13's relevant historical role is in the round-3 information-content check on a previously published 19-label circular ordering. With unknown starting point, either direction, and skipped intervening labels allowed, every ordering of three distinct labels fits. Of eight additional lists, only HT13, HT10a, and HT9b have enough distinct selected labels to discriminate under that model. The reported fractions are combinatorial properties, not empirical p-values, and the ordering was selected using the inscriptions. [H3 §3]

Do not confuse **object HT13** with **iteration 13**, the KI-RO study. If this branch is summarized in the new index, use both an object key and an iteration key. A new contribution would require genuinely discriminating unused order evidence under a frozen model or an independent administrative relationship; recalculating the short-list fractions does not supply meaning. [H3 §3; inference from the stated model and current protocol]

### ZA4/ZA15: reject only the tested settlement model

Assuming unchanged identities, comparable units, one closed interval, and no advances, overpayments, or revisions, the settlement amount cannot exceed the assessment. ZA4→ZA15 fails for QE-SI-ZU-E (4→57); reversing direction fails for Ib-NU-MA-RE (6→13 in that reversed orientation). KA-DI's 3=3 does not override those counterexamples or independently encode “paid.” The strict test excludes doubtful SI-PI-KI. ZA15's reported half-unit mismatch is retained rather than removed to create a fit. [H3 §4]

**Reopen for:** independent interval, subtype, event, scope, or direction evidence. The test does not refute every account relationship or all possible links between particular entries. [H3 §4; B2 B-D03]

### HT34: preserve reading branches and scope

Iteration 16's strong claim depended on treating the seven as an erased correction and common scope as given. The historical verifier did not respond to the state field. Iteration 17's replacement separated status, association, and accounting scope; iteration 21 and the baseline retain published 30[[7]] and 37 branches. The exact 100=70+30 complement is conditional. Even if that branch were secure, paid/unpaid, allocated/unallocated, and issued/retained remain possible relations. [H16; H17 §5; H21; B2 B-D05]

**Reopen for:** source constraints adequate to the specific comparison, or a result robust across the retained branches, plus an independent administrative relationship that separates the live meanings. [B2 B-D05]

### HT118: do not hide the scope switch

The main figures total 35 against written KU-RO 30; the final KI endpoint is 15[; missing KI under RI-RU-MA is missing, not zero. The explicit no-error single-pool model requires KI to be additional in rows but included in the final summary, and treats RI-RU-MA 10 as a modeled outflow. No observed recipient role follows. ## 4. Documentation recommendations

1. **Keep one present-tense entry point.** It should state the current mission result and point to a short current decision table before the full historical record. The frozen baseline remains authoritative for its dated historical adjudications, while current source-access and candidate priorities require the 038–041 addenda.
2. **Keep historical outcomes distinct from historical instructions.** Reports are preserved evidence. Their “next target” passages are not an automatic queue. Add a clear current override for HT100 semantic-polarity rhetoric, iteration-16 breakthrough rhetoric, and completed source-acquisition tasks.
3. **Make the correction chain discoverable by object and claim.** The essential chains are initial validation→B-D01; iteration 13 personnel→14/22/B-D04; HT34 16→review→17→21/B-D05; HT118 16→review→17/B-D06; HT9 18→19/B-D07; HT103 20→21/30/B-D08; HT100 14–15→review/B-D09; SY Za2 32→33/B-D19.
4. **Keep no-progress conditions concrete.** Repeated retrieval or a new English gloss is not a new discriminator. A new publication may still be valuable source acquisition; classify it at that level until it binds a stable element to a relation that changes the serious comparison.
5. **Retain scholarly anchors and local advances at their actual level.** “Mission unmet” must not become “nothing is known about Linear A.” KU-RO is inherited scholarship; PA-I-TO and MA-DI provide graded external category comparisons. No unique Linear A participant or action follows automatically.
6. **Do not increase apparent independence through packaging.** Different editions, agent reports, code reruns, or repeated reviews of the same object remain dependent on that object. A prospective test needs an actually unused relevant outcome, not a later training/test label.
7. **Do not rewrite the frozen historical registries to make the past look correct.** Current dispositions should explicitly control legacy assertions. The known absence of dedicated 11–20 claim IDs does not mean the reports are missing; the crosswalk supplies their evidence trail. [B1 §§5,7–8; B4]
