## 5. Why progress plateaued

The central missing link is **independent semantic identification**. A repeated label may identify a person, office, place, group, commodity category or account; several can overlap in actual use. A balance can arise from multiple administrative processes. A stamp identifies a device-use relationship before it identifies a particular person. A recovery room need not be the place or time of the transaction. None of these uncertainties disappears because an English paraphrase fits well. This is the shared implication of the audited countermodels. [S01–S11]

The project also repeatedly encountered discovery-index artifacts: lost uncertainty markers, restored forms treated as complete, cross-face fragments treated as whole words, sign aliases treated as meanings, missing lowercase variants, and document boundaries treated as linguistic boundaries. Correcting these reduces false leads. It is useful groundwork, but the correction itself does not supply an action or a role. [S01, S04–S09]

A third issue was continuity. The current 80-entry claim register preserves early historical claims and iteration 10, then resumes at 21; it has no separate iteration 11–20 claim IDs. Those reports are not absent from the project, but their correction chains were harder to retrieve from one current record. The old root verifier and manifest also belong to iteration 10, and some recent front files referenced REPORT.md or EVIDENCE_PREREQUISITES.md that exist at archive roots rather than as loose canonical root files. This consolidation adds a clear history crosswalk, current adjudication layer and explicit paths. The inherited scientific registries remain unchanged. [S12; V01]

The work has therefore progressed most clearly in source discipline and in eliminating unjustified shortcuts. It has plateaued in selecting meanings. Both statements should remain visible; describing every source retrieval or passed check as a linguistic advance would conceal the plateau.

**After access:** identify the exact carrier, any stable incised writing, and the evidence connecting that writing to the claimed responsibility. **Decision-changing observation:** an independently established object/use relationship for which a responsibility interpretation and the strongest recipient/counterparty or category interpretation predict different outcomes.

**Stop:** if the copy adds only administrative context without binding the writing to that role, or leaves the strongest serious alternative equally compatible. Obtaining the publication is an access success, not automatic promotion of its interpretation.

### Priority 2: admit a genuinely different evidence opportunity

A new candidate is welcome when it arrives with a specific packet, not simply a new name for a familiar problem. Record:

1. The exact object and written component, with uncertainty that matters to the proposed claim.
2. Two serious interpretations supported by the same context. They need not exhaust all conceivable stories.
3. An independently observed relationship on which their predictions differ.
4. The exact evidence path and a finite acquisition/evaluation scope.
5. What has already been inspected, what will be frozen, and what outcome remains unused.

A direct binding can justify a local category or relationship claim without an arbitrary minimum sample size. Conversely, many dependent occurrences do not establish a role. If the candidate lacks a known differentiating observation, classify the work as bounded exploratory source discovery and state what it is trying to locate. Do not count exploration as an admitted semantic experiment.

Use one active candidate and an independent challenger until its admission decision is clear. The challenger should reconstruct the inference and test its strongest alternative, rather than merely approve another agent's prose. Parallel source retrieval can help when the tasks are independent; extra agents do not multiply the ancient evidence.

## 7. What will count as progress

Each future result must state its actual level:

| Level | Example of a valid result | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Access | Recover the complete missing publication. | Its argument has not thereby been confirmed. |
| Reading / object control | Settle a divider, damaged numeral, object alias or physical join. | A sign reading is not its meaning. |
| Structural / physical constraint | Reject a specified fixed basket, boundary rule or production history. | Reject only the model actually contradicted. |
| Semantic discrimination | Independently bind a component to a category or relation and distinguish the strongest serious alternative. | Preserve object, event and component scope; do not infer a whole grammar. |
| Linguistic replication | Freeze a rule and successfully predict a relevant unused independent observation. | A posthoc fit or a second transcription of the same witness is insufficient. |
| Mission construction | Establish the action and relevant participant roles at the claimed level, discriminate serious alternatives, and replicate appropriately. | A status/category result is intermediate; a plausible English sentence or externally inferred physical action alone does not satisfy the mission. |

The review freezes an outcome-exposure ledger. It records **known exposure to relevant outcomes**, not invented first-seen dates. Central candidate outcomes already used in discovery cannot be recycled as unseen tests. Where historical exposure is unknown, the ledger says unknown rather than unexposed. Reading an entire new article can expose every example in it; leaving one example out of a later calculation does not restore its independence.

Before a prospective test, freeze the target component, reading alternatives, rival predictions, witness selection and success/failure criteria. If source inspection changes the rule, record a discovery-stage revision and identify a different unused test. Treat publication independence, object/event independence and independence of the semantic reasoning as separate checks.

The scientific dashboard should report the **latest decision-changing observation, current claim level, strongest remaining rival, evidence blocker and exact reopening condition**. The 80 claims, 28 experiment/feasibility records and 92 source/access records describe continuity. They include historical hypotheses, corrections and access work. They are not 80 discoveries, 28 semantic experiments, a corpus coverage percentage or a measure of how much language is decoded. [S10–S12]
