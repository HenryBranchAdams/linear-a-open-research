## CP-01: Gournia GO 2

**Source observation.** Watrous et al. 2015, pp. 436–437 and 443–446, place GO 2 (12.174) in Palace Room 16, an early LM IB destruction deposit. The report also documents metal scrap/ingot fragments in the room and proposes storage related to metalworking. GO 2 is badly burned, cracked and fragmentary. Its editor preserves several readings, including possible non-numerical alternatives. The normalized comestible/ration interpretation is explicitly qualified and built from parallels, rather than a specimen-to-text join. [S-CP03]

**Grounded rivals.** Stock of raw metal; provisioning people involved in work; another category of goods stored/recorded in the same room; records displaced in destruction. Metalworking and provisioning can coexist, so room association cannot distinguish them. An exact label-to-stock attachment or counted sealed batch would improve this comparison. Further keyword searches on the room alone will not.

**Finite inspection.** Primary report's Room 16 context and complete GO 2 entry/discussion as author-hosted extracted text; no figure pixels or object autopsy. Full-text download link returned 404. Readings are not independently adjudicated by this screen.

## CP-02: cut tablets

**Source observation.** Tomas 2013, pp. 177–179 and later simili-join discussion, distinguishes trimming from separating a longer inscription into records. Haghia Triada cut edges can leave unused space; HT 10a is cramped but is also a palimpsest. Chania tablets often fit the last line better. The published study identifies no Linear A simili-join, whereas some Linear B tablets can be physically reassembled after ancient division. [S-CP02]

**Decision.** A real paired cut surface would be independent evidence of one earlier physical text unit. The inspected packet provides no such Linear A pair. It cannot identify makers versus inscribers either; varying space fit can result from reuse or changes in planning. Do not transfer Linear B rearrangement procedures to Linear A as a demonstrated event.

## CP-03: HT 123 / information gathering

**Source observation.** Schoep 2001, pp. 58–62, reconstructs information processing from formats, find contexts and numerical/textual patterns. The paper expressly says the primary source for the tablets remains elusive and finds little evidence of direct compilation from surviving sealed documents. HT 123's proposed detailed reference and fixed obligations are inferred from its accounting relations. Incoming/tax direction further depends on interpreting KI-RO as a deficit. [S-CP01]

**Decision.** This is an important hypothesis but not an independent counterpart. Repeating HT 123's proportional arithmetic cannot validate the operation that was used to infer the missing reference. Existing B-D02/B-D03 and accounting guardrails remain controlling. A surviving exact counterpart selected independently of translation would reopen the test.

## CP-04: Anetaki handle

**Source observation.** Kanta et al., printed 2024 / online 2025, pp. 32–41, report the ring and handle in the same layer, while noting their incomplete fit. Distinct ductus and numeral/fraction usage motivate separate epigraphic treatment. The handle's accounting character and a six-fraction sequence are described; the text anticipates a fuller publication. [S-CP04]

**Decision.** Physical association does not make the two texts parallel versions, and an editorial suggestion of value ordering is not independent metrological calibration. A full exact transcription/photographic edition is necessary before a bounded ordinal or layout test. The baseline already flags sparse Anetaki coverage; no genuinely unused witness is claimed. This screen did not inspect the reported six signs as readable pixels or obtain a complete later edition.
