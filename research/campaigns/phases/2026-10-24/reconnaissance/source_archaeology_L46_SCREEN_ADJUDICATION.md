# L46 / AIN 11057: bounded follow-up and admission decision

Date: 2026-10-24. Investigator: source_archaeology. Classification: reconnaissance and reading/object control. Recommendation: **close this semantic candidate at screening for the present phase; retain a precise physical-reading continuation condition.** This is a recommendation to the lead, not a new numbered campaign or a canonical claim update.

## Question and finite packet

Could the proposed AB164a mark on an independently identified loomweight give local support to a textile-class referent, distinguish that interpretation from a syllabic/name mark, or connect the inscription to a production relationship? The packet comprises the original catalogue and epigraphic edition, their actual figure, an open recent synthesis, and four documented AB164 variant drawings. The main alternative is a three-sign syllabic sequence or identifying mark, whose first sign happens to be the graph also used logographically elsewhere. A further alternative is that the shallow first mark is not securely AB164a.

The packet was eligible before inspection: the edition's photograph and drawing might have stabilized the disputed mark, while the textile chapter could have supplied independent material evidence. It was not required to prove its own outcome before acquisition. Inspection has now reached the point where this packet does not supply a discriminating observation for the inscription's local referent.

## Exact object and source identity

The original edition identifies **L46 = AIN 11057**, from deposit **66P070**, assigned to **MC Phase C / early MM III**. It is an almost complete, two-hole discoid loomweight, reported mass **124 g**, height **8 cm**, preserved width **7.9 cm**, thickness **2.3 cm**. Its fabric is classified as imported, probably Cretan. L47/AIN11058 and L48/AIN11063 are separate catalogue objects; they are not established as an operational set with L46.

## What the reading supports

Karnava presents **AB164a–AB53–AB01**, entirely in italics, and explicitly explains that italic readings are doubtful. The first mark is shallow except for its right stroke, whereas the other two are more deeply incised. All are reported as made before firing. Direction and segmentation remain open: the central sign could have been cut first; a reverse two-sign group alongside a separate AB164a logogram is possible. Thus the editor does not simply identify one certain isolated cloth logogram.

The observed image has curved elements and a side stroke compatible with parts of the AB164 family. The faintness and uncertain extent of the mark do not reveal a clear complete enclosure equivalent to the selected comparative drawings. This is a limited visual observation on published reproductions. It is **not** an autopsy, a new identification, or proof against the editor's proposed reading. An early or cursive rendering can differ from a later standard example.

## What the new chapter changes

Marie-Louise Nosch and Jörg Weilhartner, “The Common Thread: Textiles and Wool Logograms in Linear A,” in E. Salgarella and V. Petrakis (eds), *The Wor(l)ds of Linear A*, AURA Supplement15 / ASASA(RAME)1, **2025 title-page imprint; online release 2026-01-09**, 115–135, supplies a clearly drawn AB164 comparison in **fig.6.7f, p.121**. The text at pp.122 and131 identifies the Khania roundel attestations; pp.133–134 discuss broader relationships between textile tools, weaving and the written records. It supplies **no new L46 observation, exact cloth/tool join, or local use record**. The drawing supports family-level comparison, not a new independent L46 reading.

In the same volume, Cassandra Donnelly, “Cycladic Linear A and connections between scripts,” p.68, calls the Thera object **“L36”** but cites Karnava2019 p.501 and gives the matching contextual description. This is recorded as a likely citation typo, not silently merged into a new object identity. Her document-form comparison depends on the earlier edition; it does not add an independent inspection of the first mark. The chapter describes slight incisions generally, while Karnava distinguishes the shallow first sign from the deeper other signs; preserve the original distinction.

## The grounded alternative is functional, not merely logical

Del Freo, Nosch and Rougemont, “The Terminology of Textiles in the Linear B Tablets, including Some Considerations on Linear A Logograms and Abbreviations,” in *Textile Terminologies in the Ancient Near East and Mediterranean from the Third to the First Millennia BC* (2010), 338–373, **p.353**, explicitly describes AB164 as both a logogram on Khania roundels and a syllabogram at **HT17.1 and HT19.1**. This statement was checked in the authors' [full-text rendering](https://www.academia.edu/4955873/The_Terminology_of_Textiles_in_the_Linear_B_Tablets_including_Some_Considerations_on_Linear_A_Logograms_and_Abbreviations), not just a search snippet. Page pixels and a new edition of those two tablets were not acquired for this screen.

| Interpretation | Prediction relevant to this packet | What inspection actually found |
|---|---|---|
| Separate textile-class logogram plus another mark/group | A recognizable isolated AB164-family graph and a material association with textile work; a stable separation would help | Textile-tool identity is independently supported; graph and separation remain uncertain |
| Syllabic identifying sequence | AB164 can be used inside a sign group; tool manufacture/use can carry an identifying sequence | Such use is explicitly documented elsewhere; L46 is proposed as a multi-sign inscription and direction is unsettled |
| Production or tool-set notation | A physical set, repeated mark, or operational counterpart could tie a mark to production organization | Prefiring timing is reported, but no exact L46 operational-set or named-party linkage was recovered |

The tool's function adds **modest contextual compatibility** to a textile interpretation if AB164a and a separate-logogram segmentation are accepted. That support should not be described as zero. But it does not discriminate the local referent from the serious syllabic interpretation because a person, workshop or identifying sequence can also mark a textile tool. Moreover, textile context helped motivate the original proposed identification, so counting that context again as an independent confirmation of the reading would exaggerate support. The recent chapter is another source about the same script tradition, not a second ancient L46 witness.

## Stop decision and precise continuation

No C043 semantic admission is recommended from this packet. The result is an explicit reading/scope dependency and a retained, weakly supported textile hypothesis. The accessible figure and recent chapter have been inspected; more metadata searches would not resolve the comparison.

The next potentially decisive evidence is **raking-light photography, RTI, or an epigraphic autopsy of AIN11057 focused on the shallow left mark**, preserving all strokes and competing segmentations. Once the reading is stabilized, an exact catalogue-linked set of marked tools or a independently documented production/use association could distinguish a textile class, set assignment and participant identifier. A precise request should ask for those observations, not ask the custodian to confirm a proposed translation. No correspondence has been sent.

Among the other production packets, Poursat2001's grouped Malia loomweights provide the strongest physical organization evidence, but the marks are not securely Linear A and the necessary mark-to-tool-to-group original catalogue join was not acquired. Pelon1995's Malia89P33 supplies a specific vessel-sign/fraction proposal but no independently recoverable complete carrier capacity. Those screen decisions are recorded in `RECONNAISSANCE.md`.

All target objects and comparisons inspected here are exposed; no unused validation is claimed. The new chapter changes source control and the list of grounded rivals. It does not establish a local semantic relationship or an action-and-participant construction.
