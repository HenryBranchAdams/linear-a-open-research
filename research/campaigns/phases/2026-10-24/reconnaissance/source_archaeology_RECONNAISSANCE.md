## SA1: Akrotiri inscribed loomweight

Primary packet: A. Karnava, “The inscribed loomweight,” in I. Nikolakopoulou (ed.), *Akrotiri, Thera: Middle Bronze Age Pottery and Stratigraphy* I (2019), pp.501–504, Fig.4.8 p.502; companion S. Vakirtzi, “The loomweights,” pp.485–500, especially catalogue p.491 and grouping p.495. Official volume: <https://www.archetai.gr/images/pdfs/bae/BAE_318.pdf>.

### Inspected identities and controls

| Catalogue number | Exact inventory | Deposit | Published mass / preservation | Fabric classification |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L46 | AIN11057 | 66P070 | 124g; almost intact | Imported (FCSa?), Cretan |
| L47 | AIN11058 | 66P070 | 82g; intact | Imported FCSa, Cretan |
| L48 | AIN11063 | 66P070 | 120g; intact | Imported (FCSc?), Cretan? |

These are distinct catalogue entries, not interchangeable specimens. L46 is a discoid tool with two holes and a grooved top, height8cm, preserved width7.9cm and thickness2.3cm. The deposit's date is MC PhaseC, earlyMMIII. Other weights share the deposit; shared deposit is not proof of one loom, operator or production event.

All three proposed signs are **italicized**, and Karnava says italics designate doubtful readings. This was verified against actual printed p.501 pixels and PDF font spans. Fig.4.8's photograph and line drawing on p.502 were visually inspected. The leftmost proposed AB164a is shallower than the other marks except its right stroke; the author regards all marks as prefiring but suggests a difference in clay dryness. Direction is unknown. The centrally placed proposed AB01 could have been made first. A reversed two-sign group plus separate AB164a is an alternative to one three-sign sequence. The author considers a TELA-like logogram possibility, not a secure reading and semantic identification.

**Inference limit:** a loomweight is an independent textile-production context. It can contribute graded support if the sign identification survives a shape comparison, but it does not itself distinguish a textile/product referent from maker, allocator, institution or named owner. It is also not independent confirmation of AB164a if the object function helps determine the sign reading.

**Follow-up completed:** the lead requested a bounded inspection of actual AB164 variant images and the open recent Nosch–Weilhartner textile chapter. Four SigLA exemplar drawings and fig.6.7f p.121 of the latter were actually inspected. This comparison was exposed, not blinded. It supports partial family compatibility but does not stabilize the shallow first sign or segmentation. Del Freo–Nosch–Rougemont2010 p.353 supplies a positively grounded syllabic alternative at HT17.1/19.1. Neither the recent textile chapter nor Donnelly's p.68 document-form comparison supplies a new L46 observation or exact operational join. The semantic screen is closed for this phase; a precise reading/autopsy continuation remains. See `L46_SCREEN_ADJUDICATION.md` for the source-by-source result and selection decision.

The base volume was already acquired in041 for other target pages. Inspecting this previously unread chapter is new source investigation for the relevant relationship; downloading the volume again is access maintenance.

## SA2: Malia grouped loomweights

Primary: J.-C. Poursat, “«Marques de potier» et contrôle économique à Malia à l'époque des premiers palais crétois,” *Ktèma*26 (2001),25–30, especially pp.28–29. DOI10.3406/ktema.2001.2274. Public page text: <https://www.persee.fr/doc/ktema_0221-5896_2001_num_26_1_2274?pageId=T1_29>. Page-specific HTML was acquired and read; page pixels were not inspected.

Poursat describes complete groups with32,34 and38 weights, each with one marked weight, plus incomplete groups26 and15 likewise with one marked. A145-weight group includes four weights with the same inverted-Z mark, interpreted as four looms; a later count119 must not be silently merged. Six Z-marked examples have a distinctive clay unlike associated unmarked weights. This supports a production/selection contrast, but not a named maker or transfer direction. One additional mark in the large group is postfiring and must remain separate.

Later primary comparison: A. Ulanowska2020, official Cambridge repository PDF <https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/bitstreams/ce8f9ddf-30a4-4b07-84a0-b377246df9ba/download>. Relevant discussion was read; source not a new excavation of the same objects. Her2025 “Stamped, incised, inscribed” chapter in *Meaningful Practices* was available in author's web text, while the official large PDF route failed. Original catalogue MuI45–56, MuIII330–331 remains the more useful exact next source; no request or purchase sent.

**Closure:** no securely Linear A mark or independently established administrative action was found in this packet. The unusual clay relation means production cannot be dismissed as a merely hypothetical rival. Exact fabric and object identities would have to be recovered before testing the suggested distribution mechanism. This packet does not justify C042-sized semantic investment now.

## SA4: Malia fractional vessel sign

Primary: O. Pelon, “Empreintes de sceaux et signe incisé sur deux tessons de Malia,” *BCH*119.2(1995),575–589. DOI10.3406/bch.1995.4639. Public article: <https://www.persee.fr/doc/bch_0007-4217_1995_num_119_2_4639>; page-specific text inspected at `?pageId=T1_587` and `?pageId=T1_589`.

The exact target is **sherd89P33**, the bottom of a small jug with a careful prefiring incision. Printed p.587 Fig.6 is its profile/sign; Fig.7a compares the mark with MA10b.1a in Fig.7b at1:1. The author suggests a truncated-conical vessel ideogram with an internal additional/fractional mark. That proposal does not measure the sherd's original capacity. It came from a test pit near East MagazinesXI/XII containing abundant coarse MMIII pottery. A nearby object interpreted as a measure does not establish that89P33 was a calibrated measure. Footnote62 explicitly warns that a small jug bottom is not an accounting document.

This recovers an exact original source/carrier beyond the older queue's vague reference, but cannot determine the independent quantity. The figures' captions and page text were inspected, **not the figure pixels**. Further figure access would improve reading control; unless a larger join or whole profile exists, it is unlikely to resolve the missing calibration. Close the semantic branch under this packet and retain the precise continuation dependency.
