# Independent phase-stop review

Reviewer: `/root/challenge`. Date: 2026-10-24. Scope: the three reconnaissance reports, C042 review, the finite L46 and KN Zg 58 follow-ups, and the exact prior-equivalent roundel check. No broad new search was performed. This is a review of stopping decisions, not another substantive campaign.

## Decision

**Stopping deep campaigning is justified on the inspected evidence.** The strongest rejected candidate, Akrotiri L46/AIN 11057, received the finite source and visual follow-up it deserved. It does not currently provide a stable written element plus an independently discriminating relationship. The other inspected packets either lack the exact counterpart they were meant to reveal, have an already-exposed relationship, or require a specific missing edition or observation. None supplies a concrete overlooked semantic discriminator in its inspected packet.

This does **not** mean that every potentially useful source has been inspected. PH Wg 45 remains a specific source-inspection opportunity. To challenge stopping rather than leave that possibility untested, the lead supplied the official GORILA II image route preserved in the registry. I tested the exact proposed selector 156 detail page and image endpoint. Both returned HTTP 503; no original pixels or printed page label were obtained. A different selector was not guessed after a service failure. The finite result is documented in `PH_SOURCE_SCREEN.md` and `ph_source_screen/access_ledger.json`. The remaining packet is now a specific acquisition dependency with a failed concrete route in this phase, not an inspected negative result. A bibliographic locator is not by itself a recovered source packet.

The phase can report a completed bounded investigation with a physical constraint and source/reading corrections. It cannot report the eventual mission achieved, semantic progress by file counts, or independent ancient validation. One substantive campaign is not automatically insufficient when additional candidates have been screened on materially different evidence; equally, filling three iteration numbers would not compensate for unchanged dependencies.

## Test used in this review

A second deep campaign is warranted if a materially different, obtainable packet can realistically distinguish a finite pair of serious interpretations, or if a bounded source inspection has a concrete chance of revealing the required relationship. It need not already contain a proven reading, a known historical name, many witnesses, or an answer that eliminates every rival.

Conversely, a secure object function, a repeated mark or a shared deposit is insufficient by itself when the proposed alternatives predict the same observation. The stopping question is whether the remaining accessible observations could change that comparison, rather than whether an attractive interpretation is imaginable. I therefore distinguish a failed discriminator, a missing specific source, and a lower-priority source opportunity; they must not all be called disproved candidates.

## Strongest rejected candidate: L46

The original packet is unusually relevant because the carrier is independently identified as textile equipment and has a specific inventory, context, dimensions and published image. The source investigator inspected Karnava 2019 pp. 501–504 and the associated loomweight catalogue; this review read the extracted chapter and independently inspected actual Fig. 4.8 pixels. I also inspected the new accessible comparative figures and discussion in *The Wor(l)ds of Linear A*: Nosch–Weilhartner p. 121, Fig. 6.7f, and Donnelly p. 68. After the source investigator completed `L46_SCREEN_ADJUDICATION.md`, I read that final note and inspected the display of four source-labelled SigLA AB164 exemplars, retaining its declared derivative status and exact HT/KH identities.

The follow-up does not fail simply because the edition prints uncertainty. Doubtful readings can be testable. Here the proposed leftmost AB164a depends on shallow marks, segmentation and direction remain uncertain, and the comparative figures do not add a new view of those target strokes. A renewed visual comparison can support or weaken resemblance, but does not recover the lost depth/lighting information. Donnelly's discussion reuses the 2019 source; its apparent catalogue number L36 should not silently replace L46/AIN 11057.

Even conditionally accepting AB164a does not settle its local use. A textile/logographic interpretation and a syllabic or identifying inscription associated with a textile tool can both predict this carrier. The source investigator verified Del Freo, Nosch and Rougemont 2010 p. 353 for syllabic uses at HT 17.1 and HT 19.1; I have read that extraction in their final note, not independently acquired the original page. This makes the syllabic alternative grounded rather than an unrestricted invented story. The similar deposited weights do not form an independently demonstrated written production set or cloth batch. There is no presently inspected counterpart attaching the mark to a textile product, measured textile quantity, named operator or independently known distribution event.

The tool context remains relevant graded evidence. It should not be dismissed as meaningless, nor promoted into confirmation of a sign identification that was itself partly motivated by that context. The currently obtainable comparison gives no sufficiently stable, independent observation to select a local textile referent against the strongest alternative. A numbered deep campaign would mostly rephrase those same conditions.

**Exact reopening observation:** a target examination or raking-light/RTI packet resolving the shallow strokes and segmentation, preferably coupled with an exact marked tool-set or production counterpart. Better target pixels could settle reading before any semantic test. A second ordinary reproduction of the same 2019 photograph is not that packet. No request was sent.

## Concrete remaining PH inspection

The strongest concrete unfinished **source action**, rather than a currently admitted deep semantic test, is:

1. Acquire the original PH Wg 45 entry and plate, GORILA II p. 96, and Hallager 1996 II p. 7 plus the specific direct-sealing discussion, if an actual copy or open route is available.
2. Verify HM 1535 and the legacy PH Wc 45 → PH Wg 45 crosswalk. Keep HM 1534 / PH Wg 36 separate.
3. Inspect the reverse/substrate impressions and report whether the original source establishes exactly what was sealed, incision timing, or a physical match to another item. Merely confirming that the object was a direct sealing will not bind the inscription's referent.
4. Admit a deeper campaign only if that finite inspection produces a relationship with differing predictions for a stable element. Otherwise stop at source/object control and preserve the negative source result.

This is a concrete way an omitted discriminator might be found; it is not evidence that one exists. It does not require restarting broad reconnaissance. No local file matching GORILA, Hallager or the PH target was found in the present project/phase working-file inventory during this review. The inherited registry did preserve an official route, which the lead supplied and this reviewer then tested. Its failure is route-specific, not global unavailability. The lead's single Hallager title/publication lookup found publisher sale/catalogue records without a complete-copy route. No purchase or correspondence was authorized or performed.
