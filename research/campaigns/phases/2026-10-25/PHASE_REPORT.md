# Linear A continuation through C044

## Substantive result

**The first vessel sign on HT31 now has a reviewed, independently grounded preference for a bronze-cauldron class over the particular ceramic tripod class proposed from the local kiln.** The decisive support is the combination of a curved basin, upright loop handles and long straight supports, compared with the excavated bronze vessel Malia, Quartier Mu, inventory 3068. The actual kiln rival, catalogue 5.3/c.29a1, preserves a cylindrical wall and half a horizontal cord handle, with no legs available for comparison.

This supports a local category interpretation. It does **not** prove that every vessel counted under the sign was bronze. A conventional sign could retain a bronze-derived shape while covering a broader vessel category. Consequently, excluding the whole HT31 list from an exclusively ceramic kiln consignment remains conditional on a material restriction that has not been independently established. No administrative action or participant role is identified.

This is a stronger result than access or object control alone, but it is not decipherment of a syllabic word, linguistic replication or mission completion. It recovers and challenges an existing scholarly argument; no new archaeological discovery or priority over that scholarship is claimed. [C044 report](../../iterations/044/REPORT.md)

## What changed the assessment

### C043: a meaningful physical contrast without the required writing

The first selected packet joined a published physical table and cast series to exact museum and text identities. It asked whether writing could distinguish labeling a cord from securing two cord ends at a nodule. The source paper was already known from iteration034; the new work was the exact five-carrier join.

Only HMs557 supplied a readable inscription, HT Wa1018, conventionally SI-KA. Neither selected securing-class carrier supplied a readable mark in the inspected catalogue. Fragmentary no-signs records and an absent metadata field were kept distinct. Thus the packet cannot test a correspondence across the two physical classes. No meaning was confirmed or refuted.

The challenger also corrected the physical scope: internal cord topology does not identify a particular attached container or document. The completed [C043 record](../../iterations/043/REPORT.md) preserves that correction, exact joins, failed original-edition routes and a small reproducible eligibility check. It closes at source/object control.

### C044: the original argument survives a concrete ceramic challenge

The second candidate emerged from original studies of Haghia Triada's pottery kiln and vessel account HT31. A ceramic tripod cooking-pot waster was a serious rival to a simple argument from three legs to metal. The initial brief therefore authorized inspection of the actual sign, pottery catalogue and cited bronze comparisons.

Following the citations changed the question. Duhoux2000–2001 explicitly acknowledges terracotta tripods. His argument concerns the drawn supports' length and thinness. Vandenabeele1974 adds the complete feature combination and publishes both the HT31 sign photograph and the Malia3068 bronze counterpart. The team inspected those actual pixels. These three studies form one dependent scholarly chain, not three archaeological confirmations.

The independently excavated kiln fragment is positive evidence of ceramic tripod production. Its catalogue description, however, supplies a different surviving body and handle and no supports. It is therefore not an exact counterexample. A bounded follow-up examined a same-site tripod tray and traced Duhoux's direct ceramic discussion. The tray has a different flat perforated platform and triangular feet, with an unresolved text-layer inventory discrepancy. The direct ceramic discussion resolves to Duhoux2003, but its full text remains inaccessible through the inspected routes.

The positive comparison thus survived the planned challenge. The result is a **graded preference**, supported by combined morphology. It is not a claim that ceramic supports were mechanically impossible, that no matching ceramic vessel ever existed, or that a short unsuccessful search proves bronze.
