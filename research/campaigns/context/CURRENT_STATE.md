The measurements are separate source accounts, not values to average. Rim similarity is not measured equality of whole-vessel capacity. The later technical observations ground a production-related rival without identifying workshops. Reported incision before coating/firing constrains the production sequence; it does not establish who wrote or whom the writing named. The later author withdrew the necessity of separate potter and inscriber, without proving either identity or separation. [040 report](../iterations/040/REPORT.md); [041 catalogue overlay](../iterations/041/evidence/CATALOGUE_OVERLAY.json).

The required chain is:

**Exact inventory/catalogue identity → sampled sherd or documented physical join → laboratory code → diagnostic use evidence → comparison separating manufacture from use.**

The first link is established in the inspected catalogue. The analytical link is unrecovered in the inspected packet. Hilditch's four concordance pages contain blank fields and numbering irregularities; they cannot support a conclusion that the jars were never sampled. Hilditch integer codes and the 2013 ACV/AKR codes remain different namespaces until a documented bridge connects them. The 17 positive-result ACV↔AKR pairs are a navigation aid, not target residue data. [041 report](../iterations/041/REPORT.md); [join decision](../iterations/041/analysis/JOIN_DECISION.md).

Neither a ceramic fabric category nor soot reported for neighboring catalogue 1123 establishes these jars' contents. The 2021 residue chapter was not acquired: zero article pages were inspected in 041. Access failure does not refute its argument. Later residue or use evidence would also have to be connected to the stage and resolution claimed for the prefiring mark. [041 source records](../iterations/041/evidence/SOURCE_RECORDS.json).

The pair conditionally contradicts one very narrow model: a single fixed prominent mark determined solely by the coarse G10 vessel class. It does not refute all vessel-name, capacity, acrophonic, production or allocation interpretations. A semantic experiment must compare grounded predictions, not treat all imaginable alternatives as equally probable.
