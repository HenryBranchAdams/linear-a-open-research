# Top candidate dossier

## 1. KU-*56-NU — strongest semantic negative control
It appears in three KI-RO-bearing records (HT1, HT88, HT117) and multiple non-KI-RO records. HT3 assigns 3; HT49 assigns 1; HT122 contains two entries. Montecchi explicitly uses this sequence to show that 1-lists need not be personal-name lists.

**What it proves:** our earlier structural diagnostic was overstrong.

**What it does not prove:** that KU-*56-NU is a commodity, place, office, or account. Linear B male-name parallels exist, but cross-script resemblance is only a control.

## 2. PA-TA-NE — strongest physical bridge
HT94b places PA-TA-NE 1 in a KI-RO section. HT Wa1019 bears PA-TA-NE SI-TU on a nodule. Montecchi explicitly discusses this cross-media recurrence.

**What it proves:** the designation participates in more than one administrative medium.

**What remains:** nodulus function and direction are not fixed. HT122a also has a modern PA-TA-NE reading, but the frozen SigLA snapshot reads PA-RI-NE there, so that witness is quarantined pending primary adjudication.

## 3. PA-JA-RE — strongest quantity-regime shift
HT88 has PA-JA-RE 1 under KI-RO; HT29 records PA-JA-RE 2; HT8b gives a fractional amount; ZA10b provides a cross-site recurrence.

**Implication:** same-form recurrence does not preserve one-count syntax, weakening a narrow personal-name-only model.

## 4. SA-RU — strongest commodity-account crossover
HT94b has SA-RU 1 under KI-RO, while HT86/95 place SA-RU in the recurring grain-account packet and HT123 uses SA-RU in commodity accounting.

**Implication:** administrative/account-like interpretation remains plausible, but one historical identity is not established.
