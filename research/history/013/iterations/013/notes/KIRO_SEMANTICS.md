# KI-RO semantic audit

## Why 'deficit' is too narrow

The traditional deficit/owing interpretation is attractive in personnel contexts, but the cross-domain evidence does not preserve a fixed negative sign.

Cash & Cash model HT123 as a difference between actual and reference values and explicitly note that taxation and allocation readings are structurally symmetric. Monti's published critique goes further: under the Cash arithmetic, the row differences can be positive surpluses.

Thus 'deficit' is one possible contextual realization, not the secure lexical core.

## What survives all tested contexts

A neutral functional description does:

**KI-RO identifies a differential, exception, outstanding balance, or separately reconciled section.**

This accounts for:
- paired commodity sections on HT30,
- row-level numerical differences on HT123,
- one-count personnel sections on HT88 and HT94,
- and the longer KI-RO-containing heading on HT117.

## What the personnel construction says

The secure reading is structural:

`KI-RO • X 1 • Y 1 ... KU-RO N`

means that X, Y, ... are members of the KI-RO-classified subset, and KU-RO counts that subset.

It does **not** yet tell us whether the subset is missing, surplus, owing, exempt, or otherwise exceptional.
