# Jar branch: THE Zb 6 — original publication and archaeological constraints

Research date: 2026-09-07 (session date). This branch obtained and inspected the original publications, not merely their citations. PDFs and page images remain in scratch only and must not enter the deliverable ZIP.

## What changed

The pithos has the canonical identifier **THE Zb 6** in Boulotis 2008 and the GORILA supplement preparatory catalogue. Its **complete** edited inscription is `AB 08-59-06-46`, conventionally `A-TA-NA-JE`, retained here as `A-TA-NA-*46`. The original photograph supports four preserved signs; no restoration supplies the last sign. This verifies the actual object behind Karnava's comparison.

More consequentially, the physical evidence does **not** independently identify a source or recipient. The original excavation accounts describe fragments recovered in lightwell deposits and subsequently reconstructed. The apparent Cretan manufacture is an archaeological attribution: earlier discussion expressly awaited clay analysis, and a recent review still describes the basis as macroscopic characteristics. A Cretan production site has not been identified by a material analysis retrieved in this run.

The comparison of `TA-NA-TE` with `A-TA-NA-TE`, including a possible initial `A-`, was already published by Boulotis in 2008, p. 71 n. 11. This is a prior equivalent morphological suggestion, although it does not cover our complete TE/TI grid or prove lexical identity.

## Decisive original sources

**Boulotis 2008, pp. 69–71, figure 8a–b, notes 7–11.** The publisher's complete PDF was retrieved from [BAE 257](https://www.archetai.gr/images/pdfs/bae/BAE_257.pdf), linked in its [catalogue](https://www.archetai.gr/index.php?id=67&lang=&p=content&section=8). Printed pp. 69–71 are PDF pages 64–66 (one-based). All three pages were rendered and visually inspected.

The publication describes a rope-decorated pithoid jar, apparently Cretan, discovered in the House of Ladies lightwell. It regards the inscription as intact. Note 8 supplies `08-59-06-46`, identifies the third sign as a variant of AB 06 and compares KN Zb 40.2. Figure 8 includes the reconstructed vessel, a photograph of the inscription between relief bands, and the reading. Note 10 treats the ZA 9 final sign as damaged and unclear, despite a tentative comparison with ZA 10. The printed discovery year is 1990. Note 11 suggests `TA-NA-TE` could be the same word without a possible `A-` prefix. A place or deity remains undecided in the discussion.

**Doumas, PAE 1993, p. 185 n. 32.** [Publisher PDF](https://www.archetai.gr/images/pdfs/praktika/Publ_PRAKTIKA_1993.pdf), PDF page 220 (one-based); rendered and inspected. This reports reconstruction of a medium-sized, three-handled pithos with relief rope decoration. The fragments were found in **1992** in the rubbish/debris constituting the lightwell fill of the House of Ladies. The inscription is on the shoulder, between handles. It points back to PAE 1992, p. 178. It does not identify a named owner, maker, consignee or source locality.

**Doumas, PAE 1992, p. 178.** [Publisher PDF](https://www.archetai.gr/images/pdfs/praktika/Publ_PRAKTIKA_1992.pdf), PDF page 213; rendered and inspected. The more detailed context is a thin layer of sand over the compact-earth lightwell floor; sherds of the large rope-decorated pithos came from that sand. The sand is interpreted as resulting from surface-water infiltration into spaces below the debris. Consequently, **do not turn the 1993 phrase into proof of deliberate ancient disposal**. The defensible synthesis is fragments recovered from lightwell deposits, not an intact vessel demonstrably in use in a receiving store.

**Ergon 1993, p. 90; fig. 89 on p. 91.** [Publisher PDF](https://www.archetai.gr/images/pdfs/ergon/Publ_ERGON_1993.pdf), PDF page 98 for p. 90. The text page was rendered and inspected. It explicitly dates discovery to **1990** and describes four signs carefully incised **before firing**. This supplies an early excavation-source anchor for the pre-firing claim. Its date conflicts with PAE 1993. The conflict is retained; no silent correction.

**Michailidou 2000–2001, “Indications of Literacy in Bronze Age Thera,” Minos 35–36, p. 9 n. 22 (continued on p. 10).** [Institutional PDF](https://gredos.usal.es/bitstream/10366/133892/1/Indice.pdf), PDF pages 7–8. This discusses the same four-sign rope-decorated pithos among pre-firing inscriptions, calls it possibly imported, and explicitly says clay analysis is needed to determine its origin. This is a separate archaeological discussion, not an independent ancient witness. It also illustrates inv. no. 1110 as a one-sign jug, demonstrating why the old online `THE Zb 6 (NMA 1110)` cannot be merged with the present four-sign pithos solely on the identifier.

**Notti, “La scrittura ‘fuori’ da Creta. Nuove evidenze epigrafiche ad Akrotiri (Thera),” in Lingue e scritture nell'Egeo pregreco, pp. 183–208, especially p. 185 n. 13 and p. 189.** [Institutional chapter PDF](https://www.openstarts.units.it/bitstreams/25cf919c-0a22-4a27-b609-5b7a85c8ceb8/download). Chapter text read locally; exact volume publication year was not established from its standalone PDF. It cites research through 2022. Note 13 says local/non-local attribution of Theran inscriptions rests on macroscopic features, especially clay colour, and describes THE Zb 6 as apparently Cretan. This does not establish that no subsequent scientific analysis exists; targeted searches did not retrieve one. Page 189 retains `08-59-06-46`.

## Bibliographic and visual controls

- [Boulotis 1998](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bch_0007-4217_1998_num_122_2_7184), p. 407, read via the public page-content endpoint. It describes four signs on a small jar, apparently Cretan, found in 1992 on the lightwell floor (room 9). This is a **preliminary notice**, not the full 2008 edition. Its location wording is less detailed than the excavator's account.
- [Del Freo and Zurbach 2011](https://www.persee.fr/doc/bch_0007-4217_2011_num_135_1_7827), p. 93, catalogue table, visually inspected from the public page image. `THE Zb 6` is an accepted document, with Boulotis 2008 pp. 69–70 fig. 8a–b as the edition. THE Zb 13 is the **different** pithos published by Karnava and Nikolakopoulou 2005. The latter must not supply observations for the four-sign pithos.
- [Montecchi 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780198908746.003.0010), pp. 180–182, fig. 9.6: its image of this jar's AB 46 was obtained and visually inspected. It is explicitly reproduced after Boulotis 2008 fig. 8b, so it is **not a second independent reading**. The chapter reports twelve AB 46 occurrences including damaged ones, and adds ARKH Zc 8, THE Zb 6 and PE 6.2 to the nine in GORILA. The chapter's proposed phonetic/iconographic conclusions were not adopted.
- [Karnava 2016](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/316285128_On_sacred_vocabulary_and_religious_dedications_the_Minoan_%27libation_formula%27), p. 351 notes 61–63, is downstream of Boulotis. Its provenance/destination interpretation adds a linguistic inference; it is not an additional archaeological observation.

## Substantive tests and outcomes

| Test | Required discriminating evidence | What was actually obtained | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Verify the four-sign witness and its boundary | Original complete inscription rather than an index substring | Full edition, original photograph and sign-number transcription | `A-TA-NA-*46` remains a complete edited witness. |
| Infer source case from physical provenance | A securely identified production locality, linked independently to `A-TA-NA`, or another alternating base whose direction is independently known | Apparently Cretan fabric; no retrieved geochemical/petrographic locality; no identification of `A-TA-NA` | The physical source is not the named source. `*46 = from` is not established. |
| Infer destination/owner from find context | A label connected to a known receiving party or functional transaction position | Fragmentary recovery in lightwell deposits; conflicting abbreviated context accounts | The receiving party cannot be read off the findspot. This rejects a proposed inference, not the historical possibility of a destination label. |
| Establish same referent across spellings | Shared independent associates, seal identity with role control, a named production site, or securely linked accounts | Only related spelling and approximate regional attribution | Same lexical item/name type/person remain distinct unresolved propositions. |

Pre-firing establishes that inscription belongs to the manufacturing stage. It excludes the **particular** model in which these same incisions were made only after the finished vessel arrived in Akrotiri. It does not exclude an intended owner or destination specified at manufacture, nor a maker/workshop label, provenance expression, contents label, or dedication. No recorded predicate is independently identified on this one-group inscription.

## Verdict and next discriminating evidence

This branch verifies an important witness and retrieves a previously inaccessible source chain. The strongest new analytical result is an **evidence dependency correction**: probable Cretan manufacture, pre-firing incision, and lightwell recovery are three physical claims, but none identifies the referent of `A-TA-NA` or the function of `*46`. The original reports supply less directional control than an unqualified “Cretan jar transported to its destination” might imply.

The most direct next test is a **published material provenance analysis on this identified jar**, followed by a separate anchor identifying that source locality with the written designation. An analysis finding local Theran manufacture would undermine this particular provenance argument; a Cretan attribution alone would still not choose among maker/source/destination. A second secure TE/*46 alternation with an independently linked referent and direction would be linguistically stronger.

No novel decipherment or sentence translation is claimed. The inherited morphology and place/deity suggestions have explicit publication antecedents.
