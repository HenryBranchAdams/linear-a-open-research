# ZA 9 priority investigation — iteration 9

**Material advance:** the publisher's full GORILA III pages 166–167 were retrieved and visually inspected, including the published photograph, facsimile, diplomatic/functional transcriptions and apparatus. SigLA's target sign and word records were also obtained. **The final sign remains unidentified. Neither TE nor TI is newly established.**

## Exact object and observations

- Object: **ZA 9**, museum **HM 1620**, clay tablet, Zakros; GORILA III pp. 166–167. The plate labels dimensions 6.20 × 10.30 × 1.40 cm. SigLA gives LM IB. The Younger-derived record places it in Palace XXVIII; that findspot was not independently checked in an excavation report here.
- Target entry: **.4–5**, `A-TA-NA-[•] OVIS+SI 2?`. The spelling is on physical line .4; its following composite animal logogram and numeral extend the entry into .5. `[•]` preserves the edition's unresolved sign slot, rather than filling it with a proposed syllabogram. The numeral's doubt is marked in GORILA's functional transcription; it must not silently become secure 2.
- The original apparatus on p. 167 presents a sign sequence corresponding to `A-TA-NA-TE`, followed by **“possible”**. The same page's main/functional transcriptions still leave the final slot unidentified. Thus the tentative TE is explicitly older than Younger's web commentary; the latter is not independent confirmation of it.
- SigLA **sequence #6**, HTML `index-word.html`, identifies `a-ta-na-[?]`, sign IDs **AB08–AB59–AB06–[?]**, four signs. The individual target **sign #17**, `index-17.html`, is `[?]`, function “Syllabogram”. It is not encoded as a sure TE or TI.
- Same-object controls: SigLA **#3 = AB37 TI** in the first line; **#27 = AB04 TE** at the end of `WI-RA-RE-MI-TE` on .6. These are marked `sure-reading` by SigLA. They are different positions on the same ancient object, not two additional documents.

## What the imagery actually permits

The publisher offers a default image and a 100% display. Both pages were downloaded at 100%, **1232 × 1624 pixels**, described by the viewer as **150 dpi**. This is a scan of the published plate, not a new archaeological photograph. I inspected both full pages and SigLA's full redraw (1158 × 1921), plus its separate crops for target #17 and controls #3/#27. SigLA transparent PNGs were composited on white for inspection only; no strokes were sharpened, regenerated, inferred or added. Copyrighted images are retained only in scratch and are not deliverables.

In the GORILA facsimile and SigLA redraw, the target is a roughly vertical stroke amid dotted damage near the right edge of line .4. The intact TI control is an arch/two converging long strokes; TE has a central stem with repeated short oblique side strokes. The target does not preserve those diagnostic distinctions in the consulted drawings. The scan photograph does not allow me to identify additional incisions securely. This is **not** a measured claim that all other strokes were originally absent. Damage can remove them.

| Candidate | Independent support available here | Discriminating feature still needed |
|---|---|---|
| TE | Original editors explicitly offer it as possible; a vertical remnant is compatible with its stem. | Secure diagnostic branching incisions attributable to the target, rather than a crack/damage. |
| TI | Same tablet supplies a secure TI shape; the single remnant does not alone exclude part of such a form. | Secure converging/curved companion stroke or another diagnostic part of a TI allograph. |
| Another sign | Many signs contain a vertical element; the retrieved record deliberately leaves the identity open. | Enough surviving form for an adequately broad sign comparison. |

The TE/TI comparison is a **targeted compatibility check**, not a complete sign classification. RE (#25 on the same tablet) also visibly has a stem; the unresolved trace alone does not justify making TE/TI an exhaustive list. No quantitative similarity score is appropriate to the present loss of distinguishing features. Knowing the proposed `A-TA-NA-TI` prediction prevents this investigator's comparison from being blind.

## Consequences for the larger question

1. The predicted `A-TA-NA-TI` cell remains **unfilled**. The original plate does not establish it.
2. The source check strengthens the *documentation of uncertainty*, not an assertion that TE is correct. The apparatus, main transcription and SigLA are consistent once their different levels of confidence are retained.
3. Both TE and TI are securely represented elsewhere **on ZA 9**. A claim that Zakros simply did not possess/use one of the signs would therefore fail. This does **not** refute regional lexical spelling of the particular TA-NA designation, which concerns distribution in that word family, not the existence of signs in the inventory.
4. Under either TE or TI, the entry remains an association of an unresolved designation with an OVIS+SI category and a doubtful quantity. This does not identify an owner, supplier or recipient. It does not connect the designation to the same historical entity on another tablet.
5. A new photograph under directional lighting/RTI or a documented later autopsy could resolve the reading, but even a secure TI would supply only a spelling at Zakros. The independent referent/role problem would remain.
