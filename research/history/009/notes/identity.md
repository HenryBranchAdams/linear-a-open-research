# Independent identity audit of the TA-NA candidates

The new contextual test supplies **no independent bridge identifying the Zakros and Haghia Triada forms as one historical participant**. The result is narrower than rejecting their linguistic relationship. It also locates the one apparent cross-site associate link and shows exactly how it arises.

## Executed comparison

I reread the complete contextual transcriptions for HT 7, HT 10, HT 98 and ZA 10 in Younger's GORILA-derived commentary. A literature lead added **HT 49 a.2**, which was outside the inherited three-witness TA-NA-TI comparison. Its reading is `]TA?-NA-TI • 1[` in the uncertainty convention used here: TA is doubtful and the left boundary is lost. It cannot be counted as a fourth completely preserved word. HT 49's identification as a name list is an interpretation, not an explicit human logogram. Packard's older discussion already linked HT 49 with HT 7, 10 and 98, so this is a recovered research lead, not a new inscription.

The dataset records 66 selected contextual rows. An exact comparison removes the target forms themselves, ignores single signs and composites, and excludes doubtful or broken labels from its strict version. It then asks whether other complete multi-sign designations recur in the compared contexts. No endings or initial A are removed to manufacture matches.

The live [SigLA HT 49a sequence view](https://sigla.phis.me/document/HT%2049a/index-word.html) encodes TA as a sure reading, whereas Younger underlines it. Both preserve the left break. That difference is recorded rather than silently upgrading the word: no original-image adjudication was undertaken in this subtask, and a secure initial TA would still not restore the lost boundary.

| Compared contexts | Exact shared non-target labels |
|---|---|
| HT 7a versus ZA 10a | None |
| HT 10b versus ZA 10a | None |
| HT 98a versus ZA 10a | None |
| HT 49a versus ZA 10a | None, also when its doubtful labels are admitted |
| Whole HT 10 versus whole ZA 10 | SA-MA |
| HT 7a versus HT 10b | DA-RE |

The **SA-MA** connection is real but lies on the opposite faces from both targets: `SA-MA 4` on **HT 10a.1**, versus `SA-MA 5` on **ZA 10b.3–4**. TA-NA-TI is on HT 10b; the two TE expressions are on ZA 10a. Thus an account-level network gains a cross-site connection by aggregating faces, while a face-level comparison has none. This does not prove that the faces are unrelated. It prevents describing the connection as a replicated local associate packet for the proposed name forms.

DA-RE does support a recurring association **within Haghia Triada**, alongside TA-NA-TI on both HT 7a and HT 10b. It supplies neither an externally identified person nor a source/recipient role. No matched quantity, attached commodity, seal identity or secure same-hand attribution joins the cross-site targets in this selected evidence.

## What the quantities constrain

| Witness | Target amount as transcribed | Independent commodity evidence |
|---|---|---|
| HT 7a.4 | 1 | VIR in the heading at a.1 |
| HT 10b.4 | 9 E | No securely identified local commodity |
| HT 98a.2 | J E | No securely identified local commodity; VINa on the other face |
| HT 49a.2 | 1[ | No explicit human logogram in the consulted text |
| ZA 10a.1 | 2 | No secure local commodity assigned to this entry |
| ZA 10a.2 | 1 | No secure local commodity assigned to this entry |

E and J remain **fraction-sign labels** here; the analysis does not need assumed exact fractional values. HT 10 and HT 98 prevent projecting HT 7's human heading into a universal literal head-count interpretation of the designation. A personal account receiving fractional goods remains entirely compatible. The fractions were present in inherited sources: their use here is an explicit contextual constraint, not newly discovered numerals.

TA-NA-TE and A-TA-NA-TE on ZA 10 are both followed by quantities. Their positions do not by themselves exhibit independently opposed participant roles. The first expression begins the face, but calling it a governing source heading would itself be an interpretation. Distinct names, related names, optional initial spelling, derivation and grammatical variation continue to fit.

An informative external control is `PA-JA-RE 1` on **HT 88.5** and **ZA 10b.5–6**. This supplies a stricter cross-site spelling-and-amount match than the TA-NA comparison, yet even it does not uniquely establish one historical person: the account unit and name type may recur. Shared labels are evidence for a related administrative vocabulary before they are individual identity evidence.

## Scope and next discrimination

This is a retrospective, selected-context diagnostic, with no null-model significance claim. Sparse lists can omit genuine associates; zero overlap does not refute identity. Unknown hands and commodities remain unknown. A same-seal association, if found, would still need adjudication as a person, office or institution.

The best next observation would be an independently linked administrative entity under two spellings: for example, a rare repeated associate-and-commodity packet tied to the same externally identifiable office in two records, with a independently established contextual role difference. Finding A-TA-NA-TI alone would test the formation prediction but leave identity and roles unresolved.

## Executed independent corpus scan

The live SigLA browse page listed **802 document faces**. I retrieved every face's sequence view, with **0 final access errors**, and extracted **1,401 indexed sequence occurrences**. All 802 declared sequence counts agree with the parser counts. These figures describe source coverage and extraction quality, not 802 ancient objects or 1,401 complete words. The code decodes HTML entities in document identifiers before constructing URLs; eight initially unsuccessful URLs were repaired this way.

The substring search `TA-NA` returns **16 sequence occurrences**. The target-family results are precisely:

| Face | SigLA discovery transcription |
|---|---|
| HT 7a | TA-NA-TI |
| HT 10b | TA-NA-TI |
| HT 49a | ]TA-NA-TI |
| HT 98a | TA-NA-TI |
| ZA 10a | TA-NA-TE; A-TA-NA-TE |
| ZA 9 | A-TA-NA-[?] |

**A-TA-NA-TI does not occur literally in this retrieved inventory.** The remaining nine hits include longer sequences such as KI-RE-TA-NA, A-DU-NI-TA-NA, JE-TA-NA, TA-NA-MA-JE and TA-NA-I-A301-TI. Their common substring is not an independent reason to assign a shared referent or morphology. The complete hit list, source URL and reading classes appear in `identity_results.json`.

This is independent of the inherited word-index file, but it is **not a complete new critical edition of Linear A**. [SigLA's update history](https://sigla.phis.me/about.html) says the GORILA tablets were covered by 2021 and lists new additions in May and June 2026. Most ritual inscriptions remain outside this snapshot. The only THE faces listed are THE 7a, 7b, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12: the original jar publication's modern **THE Zb6** is not covered. No absence claim is made for that jar or other omitted inscriptions.

The acquired inventory records UTC retrieval time, page hashes, author attribution and the **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** license. It preserves all indexed forms and reading classes, without exporting source drawings or publications. Its indexed chunks are not presumed complete words, and its faces are not counted as separate ancient objects. No publication priority is claimed for the contextual analysis or the bounded negative prediction check.
