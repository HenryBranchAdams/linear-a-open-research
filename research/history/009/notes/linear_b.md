# Linear B branch: the external name comparison now has an inspected context

Retrieval/analysis: 7 September 2026. This branch adds newly retrieved evidence to this investigation; the ancient texts and their identification are published findings.

## What changed

The critical text and photograph behind the inherited `ta-na-to` comparison were obtained. The current institutional identifier is **KN De 1618+7171+7174+fr.**, LiBER 3249, rather than the `KN Dc 1618+7171+7174` reference in Davis and Valério (2020, p.29). The inherited identifier remains an alias, not a second witness. No claim about the date or cause of the differing classification is established here.

The complete `ta-na-to` occupies the large, initial personal-name position in a sheep account; the place designation follows it, and a different, damaged expression occupies the upper position conventionally associated with the collector. Thus the Linear B comparison has **independent contextual support for a person designation**, but does not license moving a shepherd/owner/recipient role into the Linear A texts.

## Inspected critical text

[LiBER KN De 1618](https://liber.cnr.it/tablet/view/3249), text and apparatus retrieved directly as HTML; [institutional photograph](https://liber.cnr.it/static/img/tablets/KN/KN%20De%201618.jpg) retrieved and visually inspected. The 1200-pixel-wide photograph shows a joined, abraded tablet. No new stroke reading is proposed. None of these copyrighted images is redistributed.

```text
.A  [     ]-j̣ọ[  ] ,̣   OVISᵐ 47   OVISᶠ 37 ⟦3̣⟧
.B  ta-na-to , / e-ko-so ,          o OVISᵐ 16 ⟦2⟧
```

The spacing above is schematic; the machine ledger also stores the source text order. Dot-below marks uncertainty, brackets mark loss, and double brackets mark erasure. The apparatus offers **perhaps `[u-]ṭạ-j̣ọ-j̣ọ`** for .A. This is an editorial proposal, not a recovered collector identity. `ta-na-to` is undotted and bounded in this edition. Metadata: hand 117; J1 East–West Corridor; LM III A2; Herakleion Museum 618. There is no human logogram directly accompanying `ta-na-to` on this tablet and no overt action word identified by this branch.

The figures 47 + 37 + 16 equal 100, but arithmetic does not identify giving, receiving, ownership, or the exact meaning of `o`.

## Why the first designation is person-related and the second is place-related

The comparison rests on a wider Linear B administrative pattern, independently of any Linear A vowel ending:

| Witness | Relevant edited text | Independent contribution |
|---|---|---|
| KN De 1618 .B | `ta-na-to , / e-ko-so , …` | Target occupies the first position. |
| KN Db 1168+7168+fr. .B | `ma-di , / e-ko-so [` | Another first-position designation with the same second label, same hand and findspot. |
| KN As(1) 603+8157+fr. .2 | `… ma-di VIR 1 …` | A separate personnel record identifies the **name type** `ma-di` with one man; it does not establish the same individual as Db 1168. |
| KN Dn 1092+5379+fr. .1–2 | `e-ko-so OVISᵐ 225̣2̣ [̣` / `su-ki-ri-ta OVISᵐ 517 [̣` | The second-position label is also a flock-total grouping alongside another published place designation; .1 apparatus also allows `226̣2̣`. |

Links: [Db 1168](https://liber.cnr.it/tablet/view/3066), [As 603](https://liber.cnr.it/tablet/view/2670), [Dn 1092](https://liber.cnr.it/tablet/view/3473). All three critical texts were directly retrieved. The connection is a classification control, not a prosopographical identification of `ma-di` across records.

[Civitillo 2025](https://polygraphia.it/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/P-09-2025.pdf), pp.129–130, describes the recurring hand-117 layout: initial personal name, place, livestock figures, with a secondary name often placed above and written in the genitive. Her pp.138–140 caution that persons conventionally called shepherds could have broader responsibilities. The role of collectors is debated (p.128 n.1). Consequently **flock-associated person** is safer here than literal herdsman or legal owner. The place classification of `e-ko-so` is supported by corpus structure and scholarship; no identification with a specific modern archaeological site is required.

[Greco 2010, *Scribi e pastori*](https://www.academia.edu/44964264/Scribi_e_pastori), pp.179 and 185, explicitly includes De 1618 among the `e-ko-so` accounts and `ta-na-to` among its once-attested shepherd names. This independently predates the inherited paper, although both depend on the same ancient document and critical-edition tradition. It is not an independent second inscription.

## New competing comparison: `]ta-na-ti` already occurs in Linear B

[KN Uf(1) ‹311›](https://liber.cnr.it/tablet/view/619), .2:

```text
]ta-na-ti , DA 1̣[
```

The object is **missing**. LiBER derives its text from *Scripta Minoa II*, itself from Evans's Original Handlist. Both upper and lower portions are mutilated; the word's left boundary is lost. Metadata: Room of the Chariot Tablets, hand 124?, LM II–III A. This is neither a new complete `ta-na-ti` word nor a photographed original. The more exact apparent match to Linear A must retain its missing boundary and weaker transmission.

The targeted live LiBER search returned only De 1618 for exact `ta-na-to`, only Uf ‹311› for exact `ta-na-ti`, and no `ta-na-te`. A broader substring search `ta-na-t` returned those same two items. The API's exact mode **allows broken boundaries**: it matched `]ta-na-ti`, which is why search hits cannot be equated with complete words. These queries do not establish absence from texts outside LiBER's stated coverage (Agios Vasileios and Thebes archival documents excluded).

No named associate, collector, hand, findspot, or record type independently links these two witnesses as forms of one individual. The date labels also differ. Their resemblance is insufficient to establish a case pair.

## A conditional grammatical test rules out the easy Linear B bridge

[Melena 2014, *Mycenaean Writing*](https://www.academia.edu/7078918/Jos%C3%A9_L_Melena_Mycenaean_Writing_in_A_Companion_to_Linear_B_Mycenaean_Greek_Texts_and_their_World_Eds_Duhoux_Y_Morpurgo_Davies_A_Volume_3_Series_Biblioth%C3%A8que_des_Cahiers_de_lInstitut_de_Linguistique_de_Louvain_BCILL_133_2014), §17.2.5, p.22, describes omission of the second element of i-diphthongs and gives dative `da-mo` /damōi/. **If `ta-na-to` is an o-stem name**, the ordinary nominative and dative would both end in written `-to`; `-ti` is not the predicted ordinary dative spelling. A different stem or formation remains possible, but requires its own evidence. This conditional check uses established Linear B orthography, not assumed Linear A morphology.

Melena, p.112, also prints a doubly questioned `ta-na-to` with a conditional `/Thnatos/` reading. This does not establish that etymology. It means an unqualified assertion of securely non-Greek origin should not serve as an independent premise.

Sources: [HT 8](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mwenge/lineara.xyz/master/commentary/HT8.html), [HT 10](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mwenge/lineara.xyz/master/commentary/HT10.html), [As 1519](https://liber.cnr.it/tablet/view/2680), [C 912](https://liber.cnr.it/tablet/view/2780). The Linear A mirrors inherit Younger's GORILA-based readings; these are not independent new editions.

Civitillo 2025 p.131 n.20 explicitly discusses `-u`, `-e`, and `-i` corresponding to Linear B o-stems as possible Hellenization. This supplies a published adaptation hypothesis with multiple final vowels; it is **not a calibrated historical sound law**. The controls were deliberately selected after seeing the target, are few, and do not justify relative probabilities for `TA-NA-TE` versus `TA-NA-TI`. They reject a claim that external evidence already singles out an exclusive `i/o` correspondence.

## Verdict and next discriminating evidence

The external `ta-na-to` anchor is materially better established: its intact edited spelling and person-related administrative position are now source-checked, and its distinction from the place/collector positions is explicit. **No same-referent identity across the Linear A family, no Linear A case meaning, and no Linear A action has been established.**

The surviving alternatives are related names adapted into Greek; different names coincidentally sharing a short sequence; and a genuine shared Linear A designation with regional or grammatical variants. The missing Uf witness cannot choose among them. The smallest useful new observation would be an independently linked person/account under two complete Linear A forms in contexts whose roles differ without being inferred from the endings. On the B branch, a second complete, independently connected case form would be required before a cross-script inflectional bridge is defensible.

The notes and JSON preserve this result without redistributing publications. `python analysis/linear_b_check.py` reproduces the query coverage/deduplication summary and the conditional o-stem spelling test from frozen facts; it is a check on the analysis, not proof of a decipherment.
