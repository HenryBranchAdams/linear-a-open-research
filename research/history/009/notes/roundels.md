# Independent roundel pivot review

The strongest advance in this branch is an epigraphic upgrade of **ZA Wc 2 (HM 84)**: the latest published autopsy supports reading its final two strokes as **2**, rather than an erased **[[MI]]**. This supplies a better quantitative record and weakens any linguistic argument using an erased `-MI` here. It does **not** independently establish an action or a participant role.

## One reading improves; three semantic anchors do not follow

Keep the minimally committed representation:

```
ZA Wc 2
line 1: A TI KA A
line 2: DU KO {2 | [[MI]]}
```

The last alternative records editorial history; the latest autopsy favors `2`. `DU` is the least legible of the six syllabograms in Perna's discussion. Possible older traces beneath other signs and the trace between `DU` and `KO` must not be converted into clean additional words.

The following are **competing editorial segmentations**, not three independent inscriptions:

| Segmentation | Necessary semantic assumption |
|---|---|
| `A-TI-KA-A-DU-KO 2` | A single counted designation or longer expression. |
| `A-TI-KA A-DU-KO 2` | Boundary after `KA`; function of second expression unresolved. |
| `A-TI-KA A-DU KO 2` | Boundaries after `KA` and `DU`; `KO` used as a commodity sign or abbreviation. |

Anastasiadou pp. 40–41 reports that no visible separator secures these boundaries. She also states that `KO` is not otherwise catalogued as a Linear A ideogram; its commodity reading here is an argument from the arrangement of other roundels. Thus a proposed sentence with `A-TI-KA` as actor, `A-DU` as verb and `KO` as object would use mutually supporting segmentation and analogy for multiple purportedly independent anchors.

`A-DU` in tablet headings is compatible with a transaction term, but also with a place, institution, account, or other scope-setting designation. Its association with multiple commodities does not select an action meaning. The occurrence itself on this roundel is conditional on isolating it from the longer sequence.

## The seal-count inconsistency must remain visible

Perna describes **two impressions** (p. 31); both use the same seal, also found on ZA Z 1132. That second sealing probably closed a perishable folded document. Perna's physical observations support an administrative association, but neither its lost text nor an independent named seal owner is available. Shared sealing cannot assign `A-TI-KA` to that seal user.

Anastasiadou p. 39 first says the incised **2** agrees with the impression count, then in the “Appearance” paragraph says Zakros was stamped **four** times. Her Fig. 1.3 depicts two impressions. This is an internal source contradiction, plausibly a textual mistake. The working count should be **2 favored; conflicting published count 4 retained**, until checked against the complete rim or corrected by the author. Neither silently using four nor silently erasing the contradictory sentence is acceptable.

The two autopsies provide distinguishable observational support for the numeral reading, but Anastasiadou explicitly follows Perna. Count agreement is a separate physical consideration, not an additional independent discovery by each citing source. The annotated published photograph was viewable; this review makes no new stroke-level reading from it.

## A better exact name parallel, still conditional in Linear A

LiBER's critical transcription supplies:

```
KN V(4) 831.4: ]de-ro 1   ma-ti-ko 1   a-ti-ka 1
apparatus: ma-ti- over erasure; -ti-ka over erasure.
```

The `a-ti-ka` string is complete and unqualified in the edition, despite being partly written over erasure. The record is assigned to scribe 203, Area of Bull Relief, LM III A2. Anastasiadou, following Aura Jorro and Davis, classifies the Linear B expression as a male anthroponym. The LiBER transcription verifies the exact expression and counted-list context, not by itself that lexical classification.

Unlike `TA-NA-TI` versus `ta-na-to`, this comparison requires **no final-vowel substitution**. Nevertheless, isolating `A-TI-KA` in Linear A still requires the disputed internal boundary, and identical conventional transliterations do not prove identical reference, retained meaning, or a particular person. Even accepting an anthroponym would leave giver, recipient, owner, supervisor and other account-associated participant roles open.

## Quantitative controls and a useful exclusion

Anastasiadou's set gives the following audit. The source explicitly restricts itself to Neopalatial roundels with numerals on their faces; excluded rim numerals and earlier objects must not be counted as counterexamples within that scope.

| Object | Face quantity | Reported impressions | Result for literal count equality |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| GO Wc 1 | 5, cattle logogram | 5 | Match |
| HT Wc 3024 | 6, female-sheep logogram | 6 surviving; seventh covered with clay | Match after physical correction |
| ZA Wc 2 | 2 favored | 2 favored; conflicting 4 in latest text | Match conditional on count resolution |
| MA Wc <5> | 4 + fraction A 704 | 1 reported, perhaps 2 | Does not match either reported count under additive positive-fraction reading |
| PH Wc 41 | 1 + fraction A 707 | 2 | Earlier object, outside the chapter's scope; no literal integer equality under a proper positive fraction reading |

The Malia comparison prevents expanding the animal-roundel match into an unconditional rule that each seal impression always equals the number of commodity units written on the face. Delivery/ration units could differ from commodity measures. The covered seventh impression at HT Wc 3024 is particularly good evidence that **the active number of impressions mattered**, but correcting a count does not identify the direction of transfer.

The newest paper's all-`A` pattern is worth a bounded corpus check. It cannot establish that `A-` marks recipients: none of the four written entities is independently identified as a recipient. Its stronger claim that no other Neopalatial roundel inscription starts with `A` needs an explicitly complete denominator. SigLA alone cannot supply that denominator because it lacks one of the positive examples and treats faces separately. Hapax status also makes these words possible names, not proven names.

## Discriminating next evidence

The branch now has a specific dependency bottleneck. Another securely separated **`A-TI-KA`** would support lexical isolation; another isolated **`KO` beside a commodity quantity** would support its nonphonetic use. Neither alone gives a verb. The strongest genuinely directional test would pair the written designation with a independently identified participant in a closed package or mirrored record whose source and recipient can be assigned without translating `A-DU`. A second same-seal record with different writing helps test label identity but does not, by itself, resolve delivery direction.

No sentence construction meeting the mission's independence requirement is established. The positive result is a substantially better supported **counted ZA Wc 2 record**, an exact critically verified Linear B comparison, and a clear reason the attractive actor–verb–object interpretation remains underdetermined. These inscriptions and the numeral proposal are published findings, not this iteration's discoveries; publication priority for the dependency audit is not claimed.
