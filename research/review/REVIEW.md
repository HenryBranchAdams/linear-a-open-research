# Linear A research — critical review through iteration 16

**Review date:** 12 September 2026. This is a review checkpoint, not iteration 17.

## Verdict

The research has made meaningful progress from spelling resemblance toward constrained administrative relations. HT34 is a valuable local accounting-function result. Iteration 16 should nevertheless be recorded as a **qualified project milestone**, not as a completed, independently deciphered action-and-participant-role construction.

The strongest surviving claim is that the edited HT34 quantities support a complementary/remainder relation: 100 = 70 + 30. The erasure notation is important evidence, but an exact remainder is not automatically a debt, a delivery, or a fully directed transaction. HT118 supplies a promising personal-name comparison and a separate KI quantity column; it does not yet provide a fully reconciled account or independently identify the counterparty.

### Review scope

I read the recovered history through iteration 9, the iteration-10 atlas, reports for iterations 11–16, the intervening review of iteration 11, and selected evidence, claims, source-audit and code files. I extracted the retained bundles and executed the supplied verification entry points for **every iteration from 10 through 16**, in working copies. All seven returned success. Iteration 10 also reproduced its tracked outputs byte-for-byte and resolved its 92 historical evidence references.

I additionally tested the iteration-16 derivation function under a changed erasure status and independently recomputed the HT100/HT118 arithmetic. Key public editorial and scholarly sources were reopened. This was not a new examination of the ancient objects. I did not rerun all historical analyses before iteration 10 or independently verify every source assertion in every report. Public access to the original HT34 edition images was unsuccessful; the erasure check remains a check of the transmitted editorial notation.

## 1. What the cumulative research actually achieved

### Through iteration 10: a better evidence base, not recovered grammar

The early work explored ritual formulae, morphological alternations, seals, quantities and cross-script name comparisons. Its enduring contribution is the set of constraints it established. Similar spelling did not identify the same person; a shared seal did not establish ownership or transaction direction; matching quantities did not prove payment; and numerical compatibility depended on accounting assumptions. The TA-NA family remained without an independent referent-identity bridge. [P01]

Iteration 10 put those lessons into a reproducible search. It classified a snapshot of 802 document faces and 1,401 indexed sequences, with 220 contexts retained by the strict phonetic filter. These are not 802 independent tablets or 1,401 certified complete words. The HT86/95 packet remained a strong documentary link even with the proposed Q-form equivalence withheld. A DA-ME/DA-ZA contrast disappeared under an explicit edition-based correction, and TE-TU provided a concrete rival to matching TE-KE with TE-KI. [P02]

Those are useful results because they reduce false discoveries. The packet's arithmetic rejects a particular conjunction of identity and uniform scaling; it does not identify an inflection. The exact roster and several comparisons were already discussed in scholarship. [P02]

### Iterations 11–12: useful classification, with an important correction

Iteration 11 favored SA-RU as an entity/account designation rather than a direct commodity label. Its stronger claim that broader commodity-related interpretations had been eliminated was not justified. The subsequent review correctly distinguished a label's position, semantic category and historical referent; a commodity-derived account label can still stand before a commodity quantity. Iteration 12 carried that amendment forward. [P03, P04]

Iteration 12's better result is the QI-TU-NE positional contrast: a counted occurrence on HT7b, a heading on HT87 and a subheading on HT117, with DI-KI-SE recurring beneath the latter two. The comparison no longer depends on the restored HT94 ending. This supports a replicated administrative classification relation, with personnel-domain context. It does not determine whether QI-TU-NE names an office, institution, category, place, person/account or another designation. [P04]

### Iterations 13–15: structure survives; a stronger personnel story does not

HT88 and HT94b preserve KI-RO sections of one-count labels with matching KU-RO subtotals. That structural observation survives review. The inference that every counted label necessarily names a person does not. Iteration 14 explicitly corrected the earlier language and used recurrence outside those sections to demonstrate the difficulty. The surviving observation is a classified and totaled subsection, not a securely identified list of absent people. [P05, P06]

The HT100 investigation was methodologically useful because it exposed a precise dependence on a damaged numeral. The competing restorations produce different aggregate-membership models. Iteration 15 kept the numeral unresolved rather than restoring whichever value favored a translation. Its source audit relies in part on Bennett's discussion of the editions: this is a source-critical audit, not a fresh physical autopsy. [P07]

## 2. HT34: retain the result, narrow the interpretation

Iteration 16 records the relevant edited sequence as:

`… 100 • PA₃ 70 • KI-RO 30 [[7]]`

The reopened Younger commentary contains this notation. Double square brackets conventionally identify erased text. With the erased seven excluded, 70 + 30 = 100. If the seven belonged to the preceding KI-RO quantity before erasure, retaining it yields 37 and a combined amount of 107. [P08, W01, W04]

This favors an intended complementary quantity relation more strongly than an unexplained resemblance between numbers. It also makes a concrete data-processing point: flattening the current and erased states into 37 removes a distinction necessary for this analysis.

However, the equation alone does not discriminate between several administrative situations. Hypothetical paid/unpaid, allocated/unallocated and issued/remaining-stock partitions could all produce 100 = 70 + 30. These are countermodels illustrating the logical limit, not proposed translations. The erasure can support deliberate reconciliation without revealing which economic event occurred.

The strongest wording to carry forward is:

> On the published reading, and assuming the three quantities share the relevant accounting scope, HT34 supports KI-RO as the complementary/remainder quantity associated with a PA₃-marked portion.

“Deficit/outstanding balance” remains a plausible functional interpretation, not a uniquely selected lexical meaning from this equation alone. The original evidence JSON already makes the reconstructed pre-erasure 37 conditional on the seven's association with that quantity; the prose should preserve that condition. Direct plate examination remains desirable before treating the sequence and chronology as newly independently established.

### Novelty needs a precise label

Iteration 16 correctly disclaims a world-first translation. A further qualification is necessary: the reopened commentary already discusses the specific possibility that removing the PA₃-marked 70 from 100 leaves KI-RO 30 as a deficit. The basic subtraction argument is therefore present in a source used by the project. [P08, W01]

The project's contribution is rechecking the argument, foregrounding the erasure-state dependency, integrating it into the research history and proposing executable controls. No independent publication-priority claim for the erasure argument has been established. “Independent internal evidence” should not be confused with an independently discovered argument or a second independent ancient witness.

## 3. HT118: a promising local entry, not a fully explained account

The reported quantities are:

| Label | Main quantity | KI quantity |
|---|---:|---:|
| MA-DI | 15 | 10 |
| QA-QA-RU | 6 | 4 |
| A-RI-SU | 4 | 1 |
| RI-RU-MA | 10 | Not written |
| Sum of listed quantities | **35** | **15** |
| Written aggregate | **KU-RO 30** | **KI 15[** |

The KI arithmetic closes under the printed-value interpretation. The main-column arithmetic does not. The mismatch is not new to scholarship: the Younger commentary explicitly notes it. It is also already acknowledged in iteration 16's `evidence.json`, but not discussed in the executive report or the named-participant dossier. [P08, W02, W03]

This does not invalidate the local sequence `QA-QA-RU 6 KI 4`. It does mean that a matching KI subtotal cannot be used as validation of the entire account model. The relation between the six and four remains unresolved: the record does not independently tell us whether four is part of six, additional to six, or a quantity related through another accounting scheme.

The reopened transcriptions also retain the final break marker in `15[`. This does not itself demonstrate a missing numeral, but the uncertainty/boundary marker should not disappear from the evidence model. [W02–W04]

The personal-name comparison is worthwhile. Gulizio and Nakassis discuss the Linear A QA-QA-RU / Linear B qa-qa-ro comparison in the context of Minoan names and possible Greek morphological adaptation. This supplies evidence separate from the KI arithmetic for a **probable personal-name interpretation**. It does not independently identify the historical person or his transaction role. [W03]

The KI = KI-RO equation must also be kept explicit. Salgarella uses the abbreviation interpretation for HT118; the older commentary marks it with a question. The numerical subtotal verifies consistent treatment of KI quantities, not the abbreviation's meaning by itself. [W02, W03]

A defensible tiered parse is:

- Observation: `QA-QA-RU | 6 | KI 4`.
- Favored interpretation: a probable personal-name/account entry with an associated KI-marked balance quantity.
- Unestablished stronger claim: this named person owes four units to a specified institution.

The obstacle is not simply the absence of an overt verb. A non-verbal accounting construction could still be highly informative. The obstacle is that the account's roles and direction are not independently selected.

## 4. A confirmed verification defect in iteration 16

All baseline verifiers passed, but that is not the same as validating all research claims.

The iteration-16 `derive()` function reads the quantities by their array positions. It never reads the `status` field. I changed the final seven's status from `erased` to `written_current` and reran that function. **Every returned field remained identical**, including its claim that the current quantity closes the account.

The published failure-injection test checks that 70 + 30 + 7 is unequal to 100. That condition is true both before and after the status mutation. It therefore does not test whether changing the erasure status changes the interpreted current text. The reported successful erasure-status test is not supported by this implementation.

A separate status-aware calculation in the review behaves as expected under the encoded row-association model: erased seven gives a current KI-RO quantity of 30 and closure; written seven gives 37 and no closure. This contrast is saved in `audit_results.json`.

The original verifier also omits the HT118 main-column mismatch, and changing a main-column quantity leaves its output unchanged. Its participant classification is copied from a pre-entered classification string rather than independently derived from name evidence.

These findings do **not** refute the editorial reading or the archaeological hypothesis. They narrow the verification claim: numeric equalities were checked, but critical-state interpretation and semantic identification were not machine-validated as reported.

## 5. HT100 requires an additional conceptual correction

With the damaged numeral restored as 4, the non-KI values sum to 95 and adding KI 2 produces 97. With it restored as 6, the non-KI values already sum to 97; adding KI 2 produces 99, and subtracting KI 2 produces 95.

Thus the two restorations test **inclusion versus exclusion in an aggregate**, not directly positive versus negative lexical meaning. “Not included” and “subtracted” are not the same operation. A secure six would still require an accounting explanation before it established that KI denotes debt, shortage, absence or any directed transaction. This is an additional review correction to the “polarity switch” framing, while retaining iteration 15's refusal to supply an unread numeral. [P06, P07; independent calculations in audit_results.json]

## 6. Research quality and cumulative context

Iteration 10 remains a particularly strong reproducibility checkpoint: frozen inputs, correction overlays, explicit rivals, stable claims and byte-for-byte regenerated outputs. Iteration 12's evidence-derived structural checks are a real improvement over iteration 11's stored-verdict checks. Later reports also deserve credit for explicitly rejecting overstatements from their predecessors. [P02–P07]

The weakness is that report language sometimes outruns these controls: administrative labels become people; a complement becomes a debt; aggregate exclusion becomes negative polarity; and a passing program becomes evidence that source-state handling was validated. These promotions need separate evidentiary gates.

The later bundles are additive packages, not all self-contained cumulative workspaces. That is workable, but future handoffs should identify their required base and expose one authoritative current-state index. Retired claims must remain accessible without becoming active again when an older `CURRENT_STATE.md` is retrieved. The review delta supplied here is additive and does not modify the originals.

## 7. Recommended disposition and next discriminators

Retain the QI-TU-NE positional relation, the counted KI-RO subsections, the HT34 complementary-quantity result, the probable QA-QA-RU name comparison and the unresolved HT100 reading. Keep the original action-and-role mission open. Treat “deficit/owed” as a favored interpretation whose local and lexical scope remain distinct.

The immediate priority is to reconcile HT34 and HT118 with the best accessible original edition evidence while repairing the state-sensitive verifier. HT34 needs its erasure, row association and quantitative scope checked; HT118 needs its main-total discrepancy, final break, and KI abbreviation tracked in the main report rather than only in ancillary files.

The next semantic advance should come from evidence that distinguishes rivals: a physically or transactionally linked record fixing direction, an independently typed counterparty, or a comparison that tells whether the recorded amount is assessed, delivered, allocated, retained or outstanding. Repeating the same numerical equalities or expanding spelling resemblance will not resolve those particular alternatives.

**Bottom line:** The project now has a more constrained and testable accounting interpretation than at iteration 10. HT34 is worth pursuing. But the defensible achievement through iteration 16 is a qualified local accounting-function result plus a promising name-linked entry—not yet a fully deciphered transaction or a completed sentence-level mission.
