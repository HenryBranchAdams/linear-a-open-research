# Iteration 010 — exhaustive indexed-sequence packet screen

## Result

The broad search recovers useful linked administrative vocabularies, but **no independently supported identity-and-role contrast**. The strongest packet is the already-known HT 86 / HT 95 group. The apparent best terminal alternation in the frozen index, DA-ME / DA-ZA, is undermined by a source disagreement: Younger's HT 95b transcription reads DA-ME. The raw inventory must therefore not promote that difference into grammar.

Across every filtering layer, **no initial-A pair has an exact non-target associate on separate objects**. No exclusive terminal alternation involving forms of at least three signs has a non-target associate at face level. The only such object-level result is the inherited TA-NA-TE / TA-NA-TI comparison supported by SA-MA on the opposite faces. This reproduces the iteration-009 limitation; it is not new identity evidence.

These are bounded negative results for this snapshot and matching rule. They do not establish that the language lacks such alternations, nor that actual referents differ.

## Corpus and method

- Input: `corpus/sigla_sequences_2026-09-07.json`, acquired 2026-09-07, attributed to SigLA / Ester Salgarella and Simon Castellan, CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- SHA-256: `0512f28f757d9330b4fe13e213da6dd583b3562466b459ee77f9558a095a9f4b`.
- Coverage: 802 indexed faces, 1,401 indexed sequence occurrences, 651 catalog-object groups after transparent name normalization. The object count is a computational grouping, not an archaeological census. Most ritual inscriptions remain outside this corpus; see iteration-009 scope notes.
- Indexed sequences are not presumed words. SigLA's HT 95a `DI-DE-RU-QE-RA2-U` illustrates why. No quantities, commodity attachments, findspots, scribal hands, seal identities or participant roles are encoded by this analysis.
- Enumerate every unordered context pair. Compare exact sets without stripping syllables. Within each layer, rank first by number shared, then by the sum of `log2(nonempty contexts / form context frequency)`. The latter is a descriptive rarity weight, not odds of identity or a significance test.
- Run faces and objects separately. Face suffixes `a–d`, Greek suffixes and `-latus` are normalized; uppercase catalog subdivisions remain separate. KH 79 and the explicit join KH 79+89 are conservatively grouped. The full map is exported. Same-object face pairs are retained separately and excluded from rankings.
- For proposed variants, enumerate forms differing by initial A or a terminal sign only. Remove both targets before counting independent associates. Record whether either context contains both forms; only mutually exclusive occurrence pairs are called substitution candidates. This terminology does not establish actual syntactic substitution. Two-sign candidates are retained as a weak exploratory tier; matching one syllable is permissive.

All complete candidates require two or more signs, no `[]?+` in the form, exact form/sign-list agreement, and a sure-reading class for every sign. KU-RO and KI-RO are removed in every layer. Of 1,401 occurrences: 629 fail the boundary/syntax filter, 26 have fewer than two signs, 5 have uncertain readings, and 48 are the excluded accounting forms.

Possible logographic/composite signs, larger numeric sign codes and unadjudicated aliases such as QIF and IB are quarantined from the designation rankings. **This does not declare every excluded sequence nonlexical.** The all-complete audit includes those 53 quarantined occurrences specifically to disclose lost links. For example DA-SI-*118 may be an important designation despite the conservative filter. No analysis quietly deletes it from the research record.

## Rival explanations and reading sensitivity

**DA-ME / DA-ZA:** The raw HT 86a / HT 95b pair has five non-target associates. But HT 95a itself contains DA-ME, so even the raw whole-object version is not a mutually exclusive replacement. Root's source review finds DA-ME, not DA-ZA, in [Younger's HT 95 commentary](https://github.com/mwenge/lineara.xyz/blob/master/commentary/HT95.html). The script runs a named DA-ME alternative reading scenario without changing the inherited input. That scenario removes the apparent alternation and increases the exact shared packet. This is a source conflict, not an established inflection.

**Segmentation:** A separate scenario splits HT 95a DI-DE-RU-QE-RA2-U into DI-DE-RU and QE-RA2-U according to the same derived commentary. It increases overlap with HT 86a from three to four. A combined scenario applies both changes. These are explicitly labeled source-reading scenarios; this subtask did not adjudicate original images.

**TE-KE / TE-KI:** HT 85a and HT 122a share DA-RI-DA in the extended layer. Admitting quarantined DA-SI-*118 yields a second local associate. Whole-object comparison also gains DA-RE and QA-A310-I. Yet there is an explicit rival: HT 13 contains **both TE-KI and TE-TU**. Either aligns with HT 85a's TE-KE using the same shared DA-SI-*118 and KU-*79-NI. The packet therefore does not uniquely select TE-KI as TE-KE's counterpart. No grammatical ending or referent identity is established.

**Longer differing forms:** The broad screen additionally lists every pair of unequal forms of at least three signs in records sharing at least three labels after removing A-DU, SA-RA2, KU-RO and KI-RO. This rule does not require orthographic similarity. QA-RA2-WA / QE-RA2-U appears among the HT 86 / HT 95 possibilities, with sign edit distance two. Under the DA-ME reading scenario it has five non-generic exact associates. Other pairings remain in the output, including A-KA-RU / QE-RA2-U. Neither set difference nor edit distance aligns actual entries. Numerical and commodity constraints must decide whether any proposed alignment survives; root investigates those separately.

At phonetic face level there are 11,330 possible within-site pairs and 12,726 possible cross-site pairs among nonempty contexts, after same-object removal. There are 299 within-site and 20 cross-site one-or-more matches. These totals describe concentration; unequal corpus survival, text lengths and site composition prevent a causal or significance interpretation.

Frequency sensitivity removes every form occurring in more than 3, 4, 5 or 7 contexts, separately for faces and objects. At the strictest cap (3), only two phonetic face pairs retain at least two labels: HT 86a / HT 95b and HT 86a / HT 95a. At object level HT 86 / HT 95 retains DA-ME, DI-DE-RU, KU-NI-SU and MI-NU-TE. HT 117 / HT 87 also retains its two rare forms. Thus the strongest packet is not solely an effect of A-DU or SA-RA2. No null model, p-value or discovery probability is claimed. This is exploratory screening, not a preregistered test.

## Reproduction and outputs

From any directory:

```sh
python /workspace/scratch/9f1885bf9314/linear_a_research/iterations/010/code/corpus_pairs.py
```

The standard-library-only script writes `results/corpus_pairs.json` with input hash, occurrence classifications, exact object map, every overlapping pair, all variants, broad alternatives, frequency controls and named reading scenarios. `corpus_pairs_{layer}_{unit}.csv`, `corpus_pairs_variants_{layer}_{unit}.csv` and `corpus_pairs_broad_{layer}_{unit}.csv` supply flat review tables. The all-complete files are audits, not promoted designation lists.

Validation checked that every occurrence is classified exactly once, every shared-count field equals the actual context-set intersection, all ranked pairs exclude same-object matches, and every variant support set excludes both target forms. No network is needed to rerun the analysis. The original corpus was left unchanged.
