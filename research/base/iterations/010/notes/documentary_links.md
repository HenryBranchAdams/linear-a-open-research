# Documentary links: primary-source audit

Iteration 010 · inspected 9 September 2026 · independent research subtask

**Best investigated candidate: HT 86 ↔ HT 95.** Its recurrent roster supports linking an administrative designation more strongly than the TA-NA comparison. It does not establish an individual, opposed participant roles, or a translated action. The supposed DA-ME/DA-ZA contrast is a source discrepancy, not usable morphology in the inspected edition.

## What was actually inspected

The original GORILA I scans, pp.134–135 (HT 86) and 154–155 (HT 95), were downloaded from the publisher, hashed, and visually examined. These contain tablet photographs, drawings, diplomatic text, and normalized layouts. This is inspection of the 1976 edition, not new autopsy; subsequent joins and revised editions remain distinct sources. Ancient sign sequences below are conventional transliterations of the inspected printed signs, checked against the project's Younger-derived readings.

## Candidate DL-01: HT 86a ↔ HT 95a/b

| Designation | HT 86a | HT 95a | HT 95b |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| KU-NI-SU | 20 | 10 | 10 |
| SA-RU | 20 | 20 | 10 |
| DI-DE-RU | 20 | 10 | 10 |
| QA-RA2-WA / QE-RA2-U | 10, QA form | 7, QE form | 10, QE form |
| DA-ME | 20 | 10 | 10 |
| MI-NU-TE | 20 | 10 | 10 |

The row comparison is target-independent when the Q forms are removed: five other labels recur. All three Q entries follow DI-DE-RU. The Q forms are not identical strings: both their initial and terminal signs differ. These facts make the proposed lexical relationship a concrete candidate; they do not require assigning a case to either terminal sign.

The printed layout separates HT 86a into A-KA-RU and A-DU sections. The first contains KU-NI-SU, SA-RU, DI-DE-RU and QA-RA2-WA; the second DA-ME and MI-NU-TE. HT 95b begins A-DU. Grain signs occur in the records, but their explicit locations and composites differ. Project accounting analysis must preserve those differences rather than treating every number as the same commodity unit.

**Critical reading control.** On GORILA I p.155, the second sign of HT 95b.2 DA-ME is the same printed sign as in HT 95a.2 and HT 86a.4. The HT 95b.2 apparatus concerns an additional damaged sign preceding DA-ME. It does not posit ZA for the second sign of DA-ME. Therefore the frozen SigLA DA-ZA versus Younger DA-ME disagreement should be marked unresolved at the database level, while the audited GORILA reading is DA-ME. No claim is made about why SigLA differs.

HT 86b includes erased writing and palimpsest evidence in the edition. Its correspondence with HT 86a is therefore not an independent second object or straightforward simultaneous account. HT 95a and b likewise are two faces of one object. The object-level comparison has two tablets, not four independent witnesses.

### Rival explanations and role test

| Hypothesis | Fit to inspected observations | Observation needed to discriminate |
|---|---|---|
| Same designation with spelling/phonological variation | Fits repeated roster and final list position | Another linked pair, preferably by the same hand, under independently different documentary conditions |
| Same designation with grammatical variation | Also fits roster resemblance | Role evidence independent of the changed signs; currently none establishes sender versus recipient |
| Related but distinct people/accounts | Remains possible within a recurrent administrative group | A unique independent identity anchor, or co-occurrence showing the forms are distinct |
| Common commodity/quality vocabulary | Repeated list and grain association fit in principle | Independent demonstration that the list entries identify people, places, or commodity types |
| Same stock roster, different events | Fits five repeated labels and changed quantities | A unique commodity/quantity packet, date, physical link, or matching balance that ties the transactions |

Our inference: the unchanged companions under changed headings supply a useful internal control. A proposed role-conditioned alternation should explain why the Q item changes while the other designations do not, with independently established roles. A rule that simply renames A-KA-RU “from” and A-DU “to” would assume the result. Different quantities alone do not distinguish giving, receiving, assessments, stocks, deliveries, or repeated allocations.

## Prior scholarship and novelty control

Jan G.P. Best, *Ku-ni-su: Emmer Wheat or Personal Name?*, Talanta 16–17, pp.77–79, explicitly discusses the HT 86/95 set and the QA-RA2-WA/QE-RA2-U correspondence. His p.78 n.6 attributes the variant observation to Emilio Peruzzi, *Recent Interpretations of Minoan (Linear A)*, Word 15 (1959), p.317 n.15. Best reviews personal-name arguments but ultimately argues commodity specifications using ligatures and Semitic comparisons; he must not be cited as proving these are people. All three article pages were visually inspected. They contain no explicit two-to-one quantity-ratio analysis. Neither the repeated roster nor this Q-form comparison is a new discovery of this project. The commodity interpretation remains a rival, not an accepted translation. [Best article](https://www.talanta.nl/wp-content/uploads/2014/10/Best-77-79.pdf), [journal issue listing](https://www.talanta.nl/publications/previous-issues/1980s/16-17-1984-1985/).

Steele and Meißner list the Q pair with question marks among possible U/WA alternations (author manuscript p.11). Their discussion also warns that cross-script name equations often lack independent confirmation and that an administrative heading need not be an anthroponym merely because a similar Linear B name exists. [*From Linear B to Linear A: The problem of the backward projection of sound values* (2017), author manuscript](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/bitstreams/635fb0e4-fed6-4dd0-a369-16067170b87f/download).

Del Freo (2023), p.72, includes the same Q pair among possible spellings previously discussed by Olivier, both for U/WA and initial A/E variation. This is further prior-art confirmation, not new evidence for a participant-role distinction. [*Linear A and Linear B syllabograms*](https://iris.cnr.it/retrieve/803cafb0-92e8-4cab-82db-028a6f2fdcd8/Del%20Freo%20M.%202023%2C%20Linear%20A%20and%20Linear%20B%20syllabograms.pdf).

## Additional concrete lanes inspected in scholarship

**DL-02 — HT 117 ↔ HT 87:** Davis and Valério link the two through MA-KA-RI-TE and QI-TU-NE in headings/subheadings, DI-KI-SE 1, and their attribution to Scribe 9. This is a stronger administrative link than a single repeated label, but the potential absence/attendance interpretation depends on KI-RO and name classification; it supplies no secure action. Their larger HT 85/122 ordered-list study attributes several variants to Scribe 9, warning against treating spelling differences as grammar. These published findings are a control and priority lead; the original HT 87/117 plates were not inspected in this subtask. [Davis and Valério (2020), pp.25–28](https://www.academia.edu/44643375/2020_Names_and_designations_of_people_in_Linear_A_A_contextual_study_of_tablets_HT_85_and_117).

**DL-04 — TE-KE / TE-KI:** Davis and Valério's table 7 identifies Scribe 9 as using both variants; other scribes use TE-KI. Thus a TE-KE HT 85a versus TE-KI HT 122a comparison controls attributed hand and site, but does not isolate role. The two-sign forms remain weak in isolation. Object-level associates must be distinguished from face-level matches; uncertainty and restoration must be retained. No new HT 85/122 plate adjudication was completed here.

**Executed rival control for DL-04 — HT 13:** After the corpus agent identified a competing alignment, I retrieved and visually inspected GORILA I pp.26–27. The edition preserves TE-TU on line 3 and TE-KI across lines 3–4 as separate quantity-bearing entries, respectively 56 and 27 plus a fraction sign. Both are complete at these positions. DA-SI-*118 and KU-*79-NI are later entries on the same face (the latter conventionally also rendered KU-ZU-NI). Thus the two alternative alignments to TE-KE can share the same local associates. Shared associates plus a one-sign difference do not uniquely choose TE-KI over TE-TU. This does not refute TE-KE/TE-KI identity; it exposes ambiguity in that matching criterion. [HT 13 photograph/drawing, p.26](https://cefael.efa.gr/detail.php?site_id=1&actionID=page&serie_id=EtCret&volume_number=21&issue_number=1&sp=62&max_image_size=1), [HT 13 text, p.27](https://cefael.efa.gr/detail.php?site_id=1&actionID=page&serie_id=EtCret&volume_number=21&issue_number=1&sp=63&max_image_size=1).

**DL-03 — seal AT 125 across media:** Weingarten reports AT 125 on HT Wc 3015 and on nodules Wa 1471–1511 bearing L98, and distinguishes their scribal hands (pp.9–10). Her p.9 n.13 corrects the uncertain seal identification printed in GORILA II through Pini's identification. A shared seal offers identity evidence independent of text morphology, but it identifies a seal-associated administrative connection; it does not uniquely identify a named individual, establish a source/recipient role, or equate a seal user with a writer. These particulars were read in the article's searchable full text, not verified by new seal-image comparison. [Weingarten (1987), *Seal-use at LM IB Ayia Triada*, part I](https://www.academia.edu/28061569/SEAL_USE_AT_LM_IB_AYIA_TRIADA_A_MINOAN_ELITE_IN_ACTION_Part_I_ADMINISTRATIVE_CONSIDERATIONS).
